Why this matters · ليه ده مهم

English

Sharding is the most expensive database decision most teams ever make, and the hardest to undo. It costs you joins, transactions and unique constraints, and it multiplies every backup and migration. A senior engineer's job is usually to delay it with evidence, and when it's truly needed, to choose a key that won't need changing.

The second half of this lesson is about what a database promises after it says "OK". In this lesson's runs, 500 acknowledged orders vanished from a real MongoDB replica set after a failover, while a plain kill test on the same setup lost nothing. Knowing which settings make that possible on your stack is what interviews and incidents both ask about.

How these numbers were made. Real PostgreSQL 18.4 on throwaway Windows clusters on random ports, with several clusters on one machine, so network latency here is loopback only. Real MongoDB 8.0.13 as a private 3-member replica set. MongoDB 9.0 is the current major (September 2026), and everything here ran on 8.0. Other jobs were loading the machine, so times are ≈ and counts are what to trust. Citus, Vitess, Cassandra and DynamoDB don't run here, and each is labelled where it appears.

بالعامية

الـ sharding هو أغلى قرار database معظم الفرق بتاخده، وأصعب واحد ترجع فيه. بيكلّفك الـ joins والـ transactions والـ unique constraints، وبيضرب كل backup و migration في عدد الـ shards. وشغل السنيور غالبًا إنه يأجله بالدليل، ولما يبقى لازم فعلًا، يختار key مش هيحتاج يتغير.

والنص التاني من الدرس عن اللي الـ database بتوعد بيه بعد ما تقولك "OK". في تجارب الدرس ده، 500 order رجعلهم ack اختفوا من MongoDB replica set حقيقي بعد failover، مع إن kill test عادي على نفس الإعداد مضيعش حاجة. ومعرفة أنهي إعدادات بتخلّي ده ممكن على الـ stack بتاعك هو اللي الـ interviews والـ incidents بيسألوا عنه.

Part 1 — When to shard 🔴 Advancedالجزء 1 — إمتى تعمل sharding

English

Sharding means splitting one logical table across independent database servers. Partitioning (lesson 24) splits a table inside one server, so it helps size and maintenance, but all writes still hit one machine. Sharding is the only rung that adds write capacity, and it's the last one on the ladder because it's the only one that changes what your application can do.

Climb in order, because each rung is cheaper than the next. Fix queries and indexes often buys the most for free. Vertical scaling is a config change, and the largest instances are very large. Read replicas and caching add read capacity only. Partitioning and archiving keep tables and indexes small. The calculator below shows the pattern with growth at 8% per month. Its capacity numbers are assumptions you must replace with your own load tests. Rungs 3 and 4 push reads out by years, but the "runs out" column stays at 37 months because writes are what's left. That's the real signal: you shard when write throughput or data size outgrows the biggest machine you'll pay for, not because reads are slow.

What you give up, all shown for real in later parts: joins across shards pull rows to one place, transactions across shards aren't atomic without extra machinery, unique constraints only hold per shard, queries without the key fan out, and operations multiply: every backup, upgrade and migration runs N times and can fail partway. The hashing and rebalancing theory is in section 10 · 2.2 and 01-CS lesson 10. This lesson is about living with it on real databases.

بالعامية

الـ sharding معناه إنك تقسم جدول منطقي واحد على سيرفرات database مستقلة. أما الـ partitioning (الدرس 24) فبيقسم الجدول جوه سيرفر واحد، فبيساعد في الحجم والصيانة، لكن كل الكتابة لسه على جهاز واحد. الـ sharding هو الدرجة الوحيدة اللي بتزوّد قدرة الكتابة، وهو آخر درجة في السلم لأنه الوحيد اللي بيغيّر إيه اللي الـ application يقدر يعمله.

اطلع بالترتيب، لأن كل درجة أرخص من اللي بعدها. تصليح الـ queries والـ indexes غالبًا بيجيب أكبر مكسب ببلاش. والسيرفر الأكبر تغيير config، وأكبر السيرفرات كبيرة جدًا. والـ read replicas والـ caching بيزوّدوا القراية بس. والـ partitioning والأرشفة بيخلّوا الجداول والـ indexes صغيرين. الحاسبة فوق بتوريك الشكل ده مع نمو 8% في الشهر، وأرقام السعة فيها افتراضات لازم تبدّلها بالـ load tests بتاعتك. الدرجتين 3 و4 بيأجلوا القراية سنين، لكن عمود "بيخلص" بيفضل عند 37 شهر، لأن الكتابة هي اللي فاضلة. وده الإشارة الحقيقية: بتعمل sharding لما الكتابة أو حجم الـ data يكبروا عن أكبر سيرفر مستعد تدفع تمنه، مش عشان القراية بطيئة.

اللي بتتنازل عنه، وكله متجرب في الأجزاء الجاية: الـ joins بين الـ shards بتشد الصفوف لمكان واحد، والـ transactions بين الـ shards مش atomic من غير أدوات زيادة، والـ unique constraints بتتضمن جوه الـ shard بس، والـ queries من غير الـ key بتروح لكل الـ shards، والتشغيل بيتضاعف: كل backup و upgrade و migration بيشتغل N مرة وممكن يفشل في النص. ونظرية الـ hashing والـ rebalancing في القسم 10 · 2.2 و01-CS الدرس 10. الدرس ده عن إنك تعيش معاها على databases حقيقية.

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ الـ CTO قال في اجتماع "الـ database بقت بطيئة، لازم sharding". قبل ما تقول آه، طلّع من pg_stat_statements أغلى 10 queries (الدرس 25)، وشوف الـ write QPS الحقيقي، وحجم الـ data، والنمو الشهري، وحطهم في الحاسبة دي. غالبًا هتطلع بـ index ناقص، و read replica، وأرشفة orders أقدم من سنتين، و 3 سنين قدامك. ولو الحساب قال إن الكتابة هتخلص خلال سنة، ساعتها عندك design doc بأرقام، مش إحساس.
Trade-off: don't wait until it hurts either Resharding under load is far harder than sharding calmly, since the moves compete with production traffic for the disks that are already full. If the calculator says 12 to 18 months, start now: add tenant_id to every table and query, which is useful anyway (lesson 28), and it makes the later move possible.
وكمان متستناش لحد ما يوجع. الـ resharding تحت الضغط أصعب بكتير من الـ sharding بهدوء، لأن نقل الـ data بيتخانق مع traffic الـ production على نفس الـ disks المليانة أصلًا. فلو الحاسبة قالت من 12 لـ 18 شهر، ابدأ دلوقتي: ضيف tenant_id في كل جدول و query، وده مفيد في كل الأحوال (الدرس 28)، وبيخلّي النقل بعدين ممكن.

Part 2 — The shard key 🔴 Advancedالجزء 2 — الـ shard key

English

The shard key decides everything, and it's nearly impossible to change later: changing it means rewriting all data and every query. Judge a candidate on three things. Cardinality: there must be many more values than shards, otherwise you can't spread the data at all. Hot spots: no single value should hold or receive too much. Query patterns: most queries must already carry the key, or they fan out to every shard.

The run below uses the shop dataset: 1M orders, 200 tenants, 8 shards, where the ideal share is 12.5%. status has 5 values, so it fills only 4 of 8 shards and one holds 85%. customer_id and id spread almost perfectly at about 12.6%. tenant_id looks worst among the real candidates: 24.5%, nearly twice the ideal, because tenant 1 alone holds 17% and some other tenants hash next to it.

Then the second table turns that around. With an assumed SaaS query mix, tenant_id lets 95% of queries touch one shard, while customer_id manages 10% and id manages 30%. A perfectly even key that makes 90% of queries fan out is a worse system than a skewed key that keeps them local. So the usual SaaS answer is tenant_id, with a plan for the whales: pin tenant 1 to its own shard, and the largest shard drops to 17% (that tenant alone). A directory table maps tenant to shard, the same placement idea as the hybrid in lesson 28. A compound key such as (tenant_id, customer_id) for one giant tenant spreads it further, but that tenant's list queries then fan out.

A time-based range key is the classic hot spot: 100.0% of the last day's inserts went to the newest shard, so all write load sits on one machine however many you own.

import pg from "pg";

// (1) بيانات المحل: tenants و customers و orders بتوزيع مايل، وعلى Postgres حقيقي
const client = new pg.Client({ connectionString: process.env.DATABASE_URL });
await client.connect();
const N = 8;

const q = async <T extends pg.QueryResultRow>(sql: string) => (await client.query<T>(sql)).rows;
const total = Number((await q<{ n: string }>(`select count(*) as n from orders`))[0]!.n);
console.log("orders", total, "| distinct tenants", (await q(`select distinct tenant_id from orders`)).length);

// (2) الـ hash هنا نفس فكرة hash partition: كل مفتاح بيروح لـ shard ثابت
async function share(label: string, expr: string) {
  const rows = await q<{ shard: number; n: string }>(`
    select (hashtextextended((${expr})::text, 0) & 9223372036854775807) % ${N} as shard, count(*) as n
    from orders group by 1`);
  const counts = rows.map((r) => Number(r.n));
  const maxShare = (Math.max(...counts) / total) * 100;
  return { key: label, shardsUsed: `${rows.length}/${N}`, maxShardPct: +maxShare.toFixed(1), idealPct: +(100 / N).toFixed(1), overIdeal: +(maxShare / (100 / N)).toFixed(2) };
}

const results = [
  await share("status (5 values)", "status"),
  await share("tenant_id", "tenant_id"),
  await share("customer_id", "customer_id"),
  await share("order id", "id"),
];

// (3) الأكبر كام tenant بياخدوا كام في المية من الصفوف؟
const top = await q<{ tenant_id: number; pct: string }>(`
  select tenant_id, round(100.0 * count(*) / ${total}, 1) as pct from orders group by 1 order by count(*) desc limit 3`);
console.log("biggest tenants", top);

// (4) الحل للـ tenant الكبير: يبقى ليه shard لوحده، والباقي hash على الـ shards التانية
const rest = await q<{ shard: number; n: string }>(`
  select (hashtextextended(tenant_id::text, 0) & 9223372036854775807) % ${N - 1} as shard, count(*) as n
  from orders where tenant_id <> ${top[0]!.tenant_id} group by 1`);
const dedicated = (Number(top[0]!.pct) / 100) * total;
const maxPinned = Math.max(dedicated, ...rest.map((r) => Number(r.n)));
results.push({ key: `tenant_id, tenant ${top[0]!.tenant_id} pinned`, shardsUsed: `${rest.length + 1}/${N}`, maxShardPct: +((maxPinned / total) * 100).toFixed(1), idealPct: +(100 / N).toFixed(1), overIdeal: +(((maxPinned / total) * 100) / (100 / N)).toFixed(2) });
console.table(results);

// (5) الميزان التاني: كام query بيقدر يروح لـ shard واحد؟ النسب افتراض لـ mix بتاع SaaS
const mix = [
  { query: "tenant's order list", pct: 55, has: ["tenant_id"] },
  { query: "order by id (tenant known)", pct: 30, has: ["tenant_id", "id"] },
  { query: "customer history (tenant known)", pct: 10, has: ["tenant_id", "customer_id"] },
  { query: "admin report across tenants", pct: 5, has: [] as string[] },
];
console.table(Object.fromEntries(["tenant_id", "customer_id", "id"].map((key) => {
  const single = mix.filter((m) => m.has.includes(key)).reduce((s, m) => s + m.pct, 0);
  return [key, { singleShardPct: single, fanOutPct: 100 - single }];
})));

// (6) الـ range sharding على الوقت: كل الـ inserts الجديدة بتروح لآخر shard
const hot = await q<{ newest_share: string }>(`
  with last_day as (select max(created_at) - interval '1 day' as since from orders)
  select round(100.0 * count(*) filter (where ntile = ${N}) / count(*), 1) as newest_share
  from (select created_at, ntile(${N}) over (order by created_at) as ntile from orders) s, last_day
  where s.created_at >= last_day.since`);
console.log("range by created_at: share of the last day's inserts on the newest shard", hot[0]!.newest_share, "%");
await client.end();

  • tenant_id: 200 قيمة هنا، والـ whales مشكلة (17% في tenant واحد)، و95% من الـ queries معاها. ده الـ default في SaaS، بشرط خطة للـ whales.
بالعامية

الـ shard key بيقرر كل حاجة، وشبه مستحيل يتغير بعدين: تغييره معناه إعادة كتابة كل الـ data وكل query. قيّم أي مرشح على 3 حاجات. الـ cardinality: لازم القيم تبقى أكتر بكتير من الـ shards، وإلا مش هتعرف توزع أصلًا. الـ hot spots: مفيش قيمة واحدة تشيل أو تستقبل كتير. الـ query patterns: معظم الـ queries لازم تكون شايلة الـ key أصلًا، وإلا هتروح لكل الـ shards.

التجربة فوق على داتا المحل: مليون order، و200 tenant، و8 shards، والنصيب المثالي 12.5%. الـ status ليه 5 قيم، فملى 4 shards بس من 8 وواحد فيهم شايل 85%. الـ customer_id والـ id اتوزعوا تقريبًا مظبوط حوالين 12.6%. والـ tenant_id باين الأوحش وسط المرشحين الحقيقيين: 24.5%، قريب من ضعف المثالي، لأن tenant 1 لوحده شايل 17% وكمان فيه tenants تانيين وقعوا معاه في نفس الـ hash.

وبعدين الجدول التاني بيقلب الصورة. مع mix مفترض لـ SaaS، الـ tenant_id بيخلّي 95% من الـ queries تلمس shard واحد، وcustomer_id 10%، وid 30%. يعني key متوزع مظبوط بس 90% من الـ queries بتروح لكل الـ shards هو system أوحش من key مايل بس بيخلّيها محلية. فالإجابة المعتادة في SaaS هي tenant_id مع خطة للـ whales: ثبّت tenant 1 على shard لوحده، فأكبر shard ينزل لـ 17% (هو لوحده). وجدول directory بيربط كل tenant بالـ shard بتاعه، وده نفس فكرة الـ placement في الـ hybrid بتاع الدرس 28. والـ compound key زي (tenant_id, customer_id) لـ tenant عملاق واحد بيوزعه أكتر، بس queries القوايم بتاعته هتبقى fan-out.

والـ range key على الوقت هو الـ hot spot الكلاسيكي: 100.0% من inserts آخر يوم راحت لآخر shard، فكل حمل الكتابة قاعد على جهاز واحد مهما كان عندك كام جهاز.

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ قبل ما تكتب كلمة في الـ design doc، شغّل نفس الحساب ده على الـ production بتاعك: select tenant_id, count(*) عشان تشوف الـ whales، وpg_stat_statements عشان تشوف كام query فيها tenant_id. لو tenant واحد عنده 30%، يبقى الـ hash لوحده مش هيحل، ومحتاج directory من اليوم الأول. ولو لقيت 40% من الـ queries من غير الـ key، يبقى الخطوة الأولى refactor الـ queries، مش الـ sharding.

Part 3 — Real shards with FDW 🔴 Advancedالجزء 3 — shards حقيقية بـ FDW

English

This is a teaching model, not how you'd run production, because it lets you see sharding's behaviour with tools you already have. Three real clusters: a coordinator holds a parent table partitioned by hash(tenant_id), and each partition is a postgres_fdw foreign table on another cluster, so rows really live elsewhere. async_capable lets the planner scan shards concurrently. Section 10 builds a router in app code for comparison.

The plan shows the routing. With the key, there's one Foreign Scan on one shard. Without it, an Append over Async Foreign Scan on both. Few keys skew: 10 equal-sized tenants hashed into 2 shards gave 4,000 rows on one and 16,000 on the other, which is why you want far more keys or logical shards than servers. Fan-out costs more than the median suggests. The median went from ≈0.37 ms to ≈1.33 ms on loopback, about 3.6×. On a real network it would be worse, and the tail is the real problem: a fan-out waits for its slowest shard. In the model with a 2% chance of a 50 ms stall per query, 1.8% of single queries were slow, and 14.2% of 8-way fan-outs (the math says 1 − 0.98⁸ = 14.9%). So p95 went from ≈0.3 ms to ≈62 ms.

Uniqueness and atomicity are where it breaks. A unique constraint must include the partition key, or Postgres refuses with 0A000, so "email is unique across all tenants" needs a separate solution. And the docs state that postgres_fdw doesn't support two-phase commit. The next run proves what that means: a transaction wrote to both shards, one shard rejected the row at commit, the coordinator reported failure, and the other shard kept its row. It's a partial commit with an error message that says nothing about it. Which side survives depends on commit order, so the second attempt happened to come out clean.

export function createLab(baseDir = process.cwd()) {
  const root = mkdtempSync(path.join(baseDir, "lab-"));
  const clusters: { data: string; up: boolean; conns: { end(): Promise<void> }[] }[] = [];

  function wrap(name: string, data: string, port: number): Cluster {
    const state = { data, up: false, conns: [] as { end(): Promise<void> }[] };
    clusters.push(state);
    const connection = { host: "127.0.0.1", port: assertPort(port), user: "app", database: "postgres" };
    return {
      name,
      port,
      async client() {
        const c = new pg.Client(connection);
        // (3) السيرفر لما يقع مينفعش السكريبت يقع معاه
        c.on("error", () => {});
        await c.connect();
        state.conns.push(c);
        return c;
      },
      pool(max = 10) {
        const p = new pg.Pool({ ...connection, max });
        p.on("error", () => {});
        state.conns.push(p);
        return p;
      },
      async start() {
        await new Promise<void>((resolve, reject) => {
          const proc = spawn(`${BIN}/postgres`, ["-D", data], { stdio: ["ignore", "pipe", "pipe"] });
          let log = "";
          const onData = (d: Buffer) => {
            log += d;
            if (/ready to accept (read-only )?connections/.test(log)) resolve();
          };
          proc.stdout.on("data", onData);
          proc.stderr.on("data", onData);
          proc.once("exit", (code) => reject(new Error(`postgres exited ${code}: ${log}`)));
        });
        state.up = true;
      },
      // (4) الـ immediate معناه crash حقيقي: مفيش checkpoint، والـ restart بيعيد الـ WAL
      crash() {
        run("pg_ctl", ["-D", data, "stop", "-m", "immediate", "-w"]);
        state.up = false;
      },
    };
  }

  return {
    async cluster(name: string, conf = ""): Promise<Cluster> {
      const data = path.join(root, name);
      run("initdb", ["-D", data, "-U", "app", "-A", "trust", "-E", "UTF8", "--no-locale"]);
      const port = assertPort(await freePort());
      writeFileSync(path.join(data, "postgresql.auto.conf"), `listen_addresses='127.0.0.1'\nport=${port}\nmax_connections=60\n${conf}\n`);
      appendFileSync(path.join(data, "pg_hba.conf"), "\nhost replication app 127.0.0.1/32 trust\n");
      const c = wrap(name, data, port);
      await c.start();
      return c;
    },
    async replicaOf(primary: Cluster, name: string, conf = ""): Promise<Cluster> {
      const data = path.join(root, name);
      run("pg_basebackup", ["-h", "127.0.0.1", "-p", String(assertPort(primary.port)), "-U", "app", "-D", data, "-R", "-X", "stream"]);
      const port = assertPort(await freePort());
      appendFileSync(path.join(data, "postgresql.auto.conf"), `\nport=${port}\n${conf}\n`);
      const c = wrap(name, data, port);
      await c.start();
      return c;
    },
    // (5) لازم تتنادى في finally، عشان مفيش process يفضل شغال بعد السكريبت
    async stopAll() {
      for (const s of clusters) for (const c of s.conns) await c.end().catch(() => {});
      for (const s of clusters) if (s.up) { try { run("pg_ctl", ["-D", s.data, "stop", "-m", "immediate", "-w"]); } catch {} }
      await new Promise((r) => setTimeout(r, 500));
      rmSync(root, { recursive: true, force: true, maxRetries: 6, retryDelay: 300 });
    },
  };

// (1) نموذج تعليمي: coordinator فيه parent table، وكل partition جدول على cluster تاني
export async function buildShards(coord: Cluster, shards: Cluster[], opts: { commitDelay?: boolean } = {}) {
  const c: pg.Client = await coord.client();
  await c.query(`create extension postgres_fdw`);
  await c.query(`create table orders (
      tenant_id int not null, id bigint not null, customer_id bigint not null,
      status text not null, total_cents bigint not null, created_at timestamptz not null
    ) partition by hash (tenant_id)`);

  for (const [i, shard] of shards.entries()) {
    const s = await shard.client();
    // (2) الـ PK لازم يبدأ بالـ shard key: الـ uniqueness بتتضمن جوه الـ shard بس
    await s.query(`create table orders_s (
        tenant_id int not null, id bigint not null, customer_id bigint not null,
        status text not null, total_cents bigint not null, created_at timestamptz not null,
        primary key (tenant_id, id)
      )`);
    await c.query(`create server sh${i} foreign data wrapper postgres_fdw
      options (host '127.0.0.1', port '${shard.port}', dbname 'postgres', async_capable 'true', fetch_size '1000')`);
    await c.query(`create user mapping for app server sh${i} options (user 'app')`);
    // (3) الـ partition دي foreign table: الصفوف فعلًا على السيرفر التاني
    await c.query(`create foreign table sh_${i} partition of orders
      for values with (modulus ${shards.length}, remainder ${i}) server sh${i}
      options (table_name 'orders_s')`);
  }
  return c;
}

English

Scatter-gather means asking every shard and merging in the app. Top-N works if each shard returns its own top N and you sort with a stable tie-break: the merged top 5 matched the coordinator exactly. Aggregates are where people get it wrong. With 4,000 rows on one shard and 16,000 on the other, the average of averages said 17,935.5 and the truth was 27,949.5. Always return sum and count from each shard and divide at the end. Distinct counts don't add at all (998 vs a true 499), since one customer can be on two shards, so you need all the values or a sketch such as HyperLogLog (01-CS lesson 10, part 6).

Design joins away. Co-location puts related rows on the same shard by using the same key, so orders and order_items both hash on tenant_id. It only pays off if the join actually runs there: with default settings the coordinator pulled 2,040 rows to return 40. With enable_partitionwise_join and use_remote_estimate, the plan became one Foreign Scan with the join inside the shard, pulling exactly 40. Reference tables such as plans and currencies are small and rarely change, so copy them to every shard with logical replication; a price change reached both shards in ≈116 ms here. Reads of them are then local and slightly stale.

Two-phase commit is the textbook answer to cross-shard atomicity, and here's what it costs. PREPARE TRANSACTION does the work and promises it can commit even after a crash. COMMIT PREPARED finishes it. Between the two, the transaction belongs to nobody. The run below kills a real coordinator process in that gap, and everything that follows is the reason teams avoid this.

The orphan stays in pg_prepared_xacts on both shards. It holds its locks, so the next payment on that wallet failed with 55P03 and would have waited forever with no lock_timeout. It stops vacuum: 10000 are dead but not yet removable, and in the extreme the docs warn of a shutdown to prevent transaction ID wraparound. A restart doesn't help: it survived a crash, because that's the whole promise. And the coordinator's decision has to be logged, or nobody knows whether to commit or roll back. The default max_prepared_transactions is 0 (55000 prepared transactions are disabled), and the docs say to keep it disabled unless you have a real transaction manager. For app code, prefer a saga (lesson 42).

English

Resharding is moving data when shard count changes. With hash % N, going from 4 to 5 shards moved 80% of keys, though only 20% needed to move. Doubling moves 50%, which is why doubling is the common modulo strategy. Consistent hashing and a table of logical shards both moved the minimum, 20%. Logical shards are the practical choice: create, say, 1,024 of them from day one, keep them as small units on 4 servers, and move whole units with a directory update. Citus works this way, with a fixed set of shards placed on workers. See 01-CS lesson 10, parts 3 and 4 for the mechanics. At an assumed 8 TB and 150 MB/s, that's 12.4 hours of copying against 3.1, before throttling to protect production.

An online tenant move ran for real below, with a writer still writing. Create a publication with a WHERE row filter, subscribe on the target, and wait for the initial copy. Then cut over: pause that tenant's writes, take the LSN after the last commit, wait for the slot to confirm it, compare counts and sums, flip the placement, resume, and delete from the source later. The result: 690 writes during the move, none lost, a pause of ≈444 ms for that one tenant, and matching totals on both sides.

The true cost is everything around that happy path. Sequences aren't replicated, so ids must be generated in a way that works on any shard. A row filter requires its column in the replica identity, otherwise UPDATE on the source fails with 42P10, as the last line shows, and that takes down production writes on that table, so test it first. You also need retries for the pause, a verification step, a rollback plan, and capacity on a disk that's probably already filling, repeated for each tenant.

import { createHash } from "node:crypto";

// (1) الـ hash ثابت: أول 4 bytes من SHA-1 كفاية لتوزيع المفاتيح في التجربة
const h = (s: string) => createHash("sha1").update(s).digest().readUInt32BE(0);
const keys = Array.from({ length: 200_000 }, (_, i) => `tenant-${i}`);

const moved = (before: (k: string) => string | number, after: (k: string) => string | number) =>
  +((keys.filter((k) => before(k) !== after(k)).length / keys.length) * 100).toFixed(1);

// (2) الـ modulo: تغيير عدد الـ shards بيغيّر مكان معظم المفاتيح
const mod = (n: number) => (k: string) => h(k) % n;

// (3) الـ consistent hashing: كل shard ليه نقط كتير على الدايرة، والمفتاح بيروح لأول نقطة بعده
function ring(n: number, vnodes = 200) {
  const points = Array.from({ length: n }, (_, s) => Array.from({ length: vnodes }, (_, v) => ({ at: h(`shard-${s}#${v}`), s }))).flat().sort((a, b) => a.at - b.at);
  return (k: string) => {
    const x = h(k);
    let lo = 0, hi = points.length;
    while (lo < hi) {
      const mid = (lo + hi) >> 1;
      if (points[mid]!.at < x) lo = mid + 1; else hi = mid;
    }
    return points[lo % points.length]!.s;
  };
}

// (4) الـ logical shards: 1024 slot ثابتين، والـ directory بيحرّك الـ slots بس بين السيرفرات
const SLOTS = 1024;
const directory = (servers: number) => {
  const map = Array.from({ length: SLOTS }, (_, slot) => slot % 4);
  for (let server = 4; server < servers; server++) {
    const want = Math.floor(SLOTS / servers);
    for (let slot = 0, taken = 0; slot < SLOTS && taken < want; slot += 4 + 1) { map[slot] = server; taken++; }
  }
  return (k: string) => map[h(k) % SLOTS]!;
};

بالعامية

الـ scatter-gather معناه تسأل كل shard وتدمج في الـ app. الـ top-N بيشتغل لو كل shard رجّع أحسن N عنده ورتبت بكسر تعادل ثابت: الـ top 5 المدموج طابق الـ coordinator بالظبط. والـ aggregates هي اللي الناس بتغلط فيها. مع 4,000 صف على shard و16,000 على التاني، متوسط المتوسطات قال 17,935.5 والحقيقة 27,949.5. رجّع دايمًا sum وcount من كل shard واقسم في الآخر. والـ distinct مبيتجمعش أصلًا (998 ضد 499 حقيقي)، لأن العميل الواحد ممكن يبقى على shardين، فمحتاج كل القيم أو sketch زي HyperLogLog (01-CS الدرس 10، الجزء 6).

صمّم عشان تتجنب الـ joins. الـ co-location بيحط الصفوف المرتبطة على نفس الـ shard باستخدام نفس الـ key، فـ orders وorder_items الاتنين hash على tenant_id. وده بيفيد بس لو الـ join اتنفذ هناك فعلًا: بالـ defaults الـ coordinator شد 2,040 صف عشان يرجّع 40. ومع enable_partitionwise_join وuse_remote_estimate الـ plan بقى Foreign Scan واحد والـ join جوه الـ shard، وشد 40 بالظبط. والـ reference tables زي الـ plans والعملات صغيرة ونادرًا ما بتتغير، فانسخها على كل shard بـ logical replication؛ تغيير السعر وصل للـ shardين في ≈116 ms هنا. والقراية منها بعد كده محلية وقديمة شوية.

الـ two-phase commit هو إجابة الكتب لموضوع الـ atomicity بين الـ shards، وده تمنه. الـ PREPARE TRANSACTION بيعمل الشغل ويوعد إنه يقدر يعمل commit حتى بعد crash. والـ COMMIT PREPARED بيخلّصه. وبينهم الـ transaction ملهاش صاحب. التجربة فوق بتموّت coordinator حقيقي في الفجوة دي، وكل اللي بعد كده هو سبب إن الفرق بتتجنب ده.

اليتيمة بتفضل في pg_prepared_xacts على الـ shardين. بتمسك الـ locks، فالدفعة الجاية على المحفظة دي فشلت بـ 55P03، ومن غير lock_timeout كانت هتستنى للأبد. وبتوقف الـ vacuum: 10000 are dead but not yet removable، وفي الحالة القصوى الدوكيومنتيشن بيحذّر من shutdown عشان transaction ID wraparound. والـ restart مبيحلّهاش: استحملت الـ crash، لأن ده الوعد نفسه. وقرار الـ coordinator لازم يتسجل، وإلا محدش يعرف يعمل commit ولا rollback. والـ default بتاع max_prepared_transactions هو 0 (55000 prepared transactions are disabled)، والدوكيومنتيشن بيقول سيبه مقفول إلا لو عندك transaction manager حقيقي. وفي كود الـ app، الأفضل saga (الدرس 42).

الـ resharding هو نقل الـ data لما عدد الـ shards يتغير. مع hash % N، من 4 لـ 5 shards نقل 80% من المفاتيح، مع إن 20% بس كانوا محتاجين يتنقلوا. والمضاعفة بتنقل 50%، وعشان كده المضاعفة هي استراتيجية الـ modulo المعتادة. والـ consistent hashing وجدول logical shards نقلوا الحد الأدنى، 20%. والـ logical shards هو الاختيار العملي: اعمل مثلًا 1,024 من أول يوم، وخليهم وحدات صغيرة على 4 سيرفرات، وانقل وحدات كاملة بتعديل directory. و Citus شغال بالطريقة دي: عدد ثابت من الـ shards موزعين على الـ workers. والتفاصيل في 01-CS الدرس 10، الجزئين 3 و4. وعلى 8 TB و150 MB/s (افتراض) يعني 12.4 ساعة نسخ ضد 3.1، قبل أي throttling لحماية الـ production.

نقل tenant أونلاين اشتغل بجد فوق، والـ writer شغال بيكتب. اعمل publication بـ WHERE row filter، واعمل subscribe على الهدف، واستنى الـ initial copy. وبعدين الـ cutover: وقّف كتابة الـ tenant ده، خد الـ LSN بعد آخر commit، استنى الـ slot يأكده، قارن العدد والمجموع، بدّل الـ placement، رجّع الكتابة، وامسح من المصدر بعدين. النتيجة: 690 كتابة أثناء النقل، مفيش حاجة ضاعت، ووقفة ≈444 ms للـ tenant ده بس، والمجاميع متطابقة.

التكلفة الحقيقية هي كل اللي حوالين السيناريو السعيد ده. الـ sequences مبتتنسخش، فالـ ids لازم تتولد بطريقة تشتغل على أي shard. والـ row filter محتاج عموده يبقى في الـ replica identity، وإلا الـ UPDATE على المصدر بيفشل بـ 42P10 زي آخر سطر، وده بيوقّع كتابة production على الجدول ده، فجرّبه الأول. ومحتاج كمان retries للوقفة، وخطوة مراجعة، وخطة رجوع، ومساحة على disk غالبًا بيتملي أصلًا، وكل ده مكرر لكل tenant.

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ tenant كبير بدأ يأثر على الباقي على shard مشترك، وعايز تنقله لـ shard لوحده من غير downtime. الخطوات هي نفس الكود فوق: publication بـ filter، و subscription، و feature flag بيوقف كتابة الـ tenant ده لأقل من ثانية، وplacement table بيتحدث، و job بيمسح المصدر بعد أسبوع. والمراجعة قبل الـ flip لازم تكون count وsum لكل جدول. والغلطة الشائعة: تاخد الـ LSN قبل آخر commit فتحوّل قبل ما الهدف يوصل، وزي ما اتشرح في الدرس 23 الـ LSN لازم يتاخد بعد الـ commit.
Interview question "Should we use two-phase commit for a transfer between two shards?" → Rarely. 2PC is atomic but blocking: a coordinator crash between PREPARE and COMMIT leaves locks held and vacuum stalled on both shards, surviving restarts, until a recovery process with a durable decision log resolves it. It also adds round trips to every write. First try to design it away by co-locating both wallets. If you can't, use a saga with idempotent steps and compensation, and accept temporary inconsistency. Use 2PC inside a system built for it, such as Citus for reference tables.
"نستخدم two-phase commit لتحويل بين shardين؟" ← نادرًا. الـ 2PC atomic لكنه blocking: crash للـ coordinator بين الـ PREPARE والـ COMMIT بيسيب locks ماسكة و vacuum واقف على الـ shardين، حتى بعد restart، لحد ما recovery process عنده decision log دائم يحلها. وكمان بيزوّد round trips لكل كتابة. جرّب الأول تصممها بعيد عنه بإنك تحط المحفظتين على نفس الـ shard. ولو مينفعش، استخدم saga بخطوات idempotent وتعويض، واقبل inconsistency مؤقت. والـ 2PC مكانه جوه system معمول له، زي Citus مع الـ reference tables.

English

Not run here: Citus and Vitess aren't installed on this machine. Commands below come from their docs, with no invented output.

Citus is a Postgres extension that turns several servers into one distributed database. Version 14.0 (February 2026) brought PostgreSQL 18 support and dropped PG 15. You call create_distributed_table('orders', 'tenant_id'), and it hashes rows into shards across workers. colocate_with keeps related tables on the same placement, and create_reference_table copies a table to every worker, using two-phase commit for changes to it. citus_rebalance_start() moves shards using logical replication, with a brief write lock at the end, per its docs.

The SQL becomes restricted, and this is the part teams underestimate. From the docs' limits page (labelled 13.0.1 when read, so check the version you run): uniqueness and primary keys must include the distribution column, and foreign keys between distributed tables must too. Outer joins between distributed tables work only on the distribution column, recursive CTEs and SELECT ... FOR UPDATE only in single-shard queries, and correlated subqueries only when correlated on the distribution column. Your data type for that column can't change later.

Vitess does this for MySQL from outside, as a proxy layer. Apps connect to VTGate over the MySQL protocol, VTTablet sits beside each MySQL instance, and a topology service holds cluster state. The shard key is a Vindex, which maps a column value to a keyspace ID, and each shard owns a range of those IDs. The Primary Vindex must be unique and its column can't be updated. Without a usable Vindex, a query scatters to all shards, and a Lookup Vindex is a cross-shard index that avoids that for another column, at the cost of another table. Online resharding is a workflow: copy, replicate changes, verify with VDiff, switch traffic with a brief write pause, then complete, which is destructive. Current stable docs are v24 from April 2026.

What stays your problem with either one: choosing the key, keeping queries on it, the whales, cross-shard reports, and the operations of many database servers. Tools move shards for you, but they can't fix a wrong key. Managed offerings exist as a category: Azure's elastic clusters are managed Citus, PlanetScale offers managed Vitess, and AWS has Aurora PostgreSQL Limitless Database. Compare them on their own docs, because this market moves fast.

English

"Is it consistent?" has no answer until you say which database, which node you read from, and which settings. The models themselves are in section 10 · 2.2. Here's what each store actually does.

PostgreSQL is strongly consistent on one node: after commit, every new statement sees it. Replicas give you anything from stale to synchronous, chosen per transaction with synchronous_commit. The test below uses a sync standby deliberately delayed by 100 ms, to separate "WAL arrived" from "WAL applied". With on, the commit waited for the standby to flush and took ≈1.86 ms, yet a read straight after was stale 60 times out of 60. Only remote_apply gave read-your-writes (0/60), and it paid the whole delay at ≈110 ms per commit. So synchronous replication protects durability, and only remote_apply protects reads. Details in lesson 23.

MongoDB is tunable per operation, and its implicit default for a replica set is w: "majority" with read concern local (real, from getDefaultRWConcern). Mongo 9.0 is current, and the tests ran on 8.0.13 with 3 local members. w:1 took ≈1.18 ms per write against ≈5.91 ms for majority. Look at w:3, though: ≈2.48 ms, faster than majority, even though it waits for all three. That's because w: "majority" implies journaling by default through writeConcernMajorityJournalDefault, while w: <number> with j unspecified only requires the write in memory. A bigger number isn't automatically stronger.

Read concerns: local and available return what the node has, which may roll back. majority returns what a majority acknowledged. linearizable reflects all majority-acknowledged writes before the read, works on the primary only with a single-document filter, and needs maxTimeMS: it cost ≈9× here (1,233 ms vs ≈140 ms per 200). snapshot reads a point in time. The lab below starts private mongod processes through a helper on this machine, and 3 Compose services do the same on yours. Reads from a secondary right after a w:1 write were stale 298 of 300 times, and 14 of 300 even after w: "majority", because the secondary you read may not be in the majority that acknowledged. A causal session with majority on both sides gave 0 of 300, and the docs say only that combination keeps its guarantees through failures.

Then lag, the real trigger, was added using a test-only failpoint on a private instance: stopReplProducer stops secondaries from pulling the oplog, while heartbeats keep the primary in charge. The document sat on the primary and could have survived or not, so the app must treat that error as "unknown", not "failed". The rollback files aren't applied back automatically.

// (1) بنختار عشوائيًا مين يوصله الكلام: زي الشبكة الحقيقية، مفيش replica مضمون
function pick(k: number): Set<number> {
  const all = [0, 1, 2];
  for (let i = all.length - 1; i > 0; i--) {
    const j = Math.floor(Math.random() * (i + 1));
    [all[i], all[j]] = [all[j]!, all[i]!];
  }
  return new Set(all.slice(0, k));
}

function staleRate(w: number, r: number) {
  let stale = 0;
  for (let t = 0; t < TRIALS; t++) {
    const wrote = pick(w), read = pick(r);
    // (2) القراءة بتبقى قديمة لو مفيش replica مشترك بين اللي كتبوا واللي قروا
    if (![...read].some((x) => wrote.has(x))) stale++;
  }
  return +((stale / TRIALS) * 100).toFixed(1);
}

Part 7 — CAP for your databases 🔴 Advancedالجزء 7 — الـ CAP للـ databases بتاعتك

English

CAP says that during a network partition, a system must choose between consistency (reject or wait) and availability (answer, possibly wrongly). There's no "CA" option in a real network, because partitions aren't optional. And the choice isn't really a label on a product: it's per operation and per setting. The theory, with linearizability, is in section 10 · 2.2.

Here it is on a real replica set: 2 of 3 members killed, which from the survivor's view is the same as being cut off. It goes through 3 stages. First ≈10 s: it still believes it's primary. A w:1 write was acknowledged, the worst moment, since that write may be rolled back when the others return. A majority write timed out with error 64. After ≈9.8 s: it steps down to secondary, so a minority can't accept writes and there can't be two primaries. Writes fail with 10107 NotWritablePrimary, and the normal client can't find a primary at all. That's consistency over availability. Reads still work if you allow a secondary, and they returned lonely-w1 and lonely-majority, data that may later vanish. Linearizable reads refuse. So one database gives you CP writes and, by choice of read preference, AP reads.

PACELC adds the case you live in every day: if there's a Partition, choose Availability or Consistency; Else, choose Latency or Consistency. Your numbers from Part 6 are exactly that trade with no failure involved: Mongo at ≈1.18 ms vs ≈5.91 ms per write, Postgres at ≈1.86 ms vs ≈110 ms when you demand reads on a lagging replica. Other stores: Postgres async failover can lose recent commits (lesson 23), and Redis replication is asynchronous, so failover can lose acknowledged writes too (lesson 38).

الـ CAP بيقول إن وقت partition في الـ network، الـ system لازم يختار بين consistency (يرفض أو يستنى) وavailability (يرد، وممكن يبقى غلط). مفيش اختيار "CA" في network حقيقية، لأن الـ partitions مش اختيارية. والاختيار مش label على المنتج أصلًا: هو لكل عملية ولكل إعداد. والنظرية، مع الـ linearizability، في القسم 10 · 2.2.

الـ PACELC بيضيف الحالة اللي عايش فيها كل يوم: لو فيه Partition اختار Availability أو Consistency؛ وإلا (Else) اختار Latency أو Consistency. أرقامك من الجزء 6 هي بالظبط المقايضة دي من غير أي عطل: Mongo ≈1.18 ضد ≈5.91 ms للكتابة، و Postgres ≈1.86 ضد ≈110 ms لما تطلب قراية مضمونة على replica متأخر. وباقي الـ stores: الـ async failover في Postgres ممكن يضيّع commits حديثة (الدرس 23)، والـ replication في Redis async، فالـ failover ممكن يضيّع writes رجعلها ack برضه (الدرس 38).

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ في design review حد قال "إحنا استخدمنا Mongo عشان AP". صحّحها بأدب: كتابة Mongo الافتراضية بتقف وقت الـ partition. واللي يفرق هو تصنيف الـ features: الرصيد والدفع يقفوا ويرجعوا error واضح (consistency)، وصفحة المنتج تعرض نسخة قديمة من secondary أو cache (availability)، و counter المشاهدات ممكن يضيع جزء منه عادي. ويتكتب ده في الـ design doc: كل feature بتختار إيه، والـ error اللي المستخدم هيشوفه وقت الـ 10 ثواني بتوع الـ failover.
Interview question "Is MongoDB CP or AP?" → The honest answer is that it depends on the operation. By default, writes are CP: a minority primary steps down within about 10 s and writes fail, as in this run. But w:1 during that window acknowledges writes that can later roll back, and a real test lost all 500 with lag present. Reads are your choice: from a secondary they stay available but can be stale, while linearizable refuses. PACELC describes daily life better: normally I'm trading latency against consistency, about 1 ms vs 6 ms per write in my test. So I'd pick per feature: money gets majority, analytics can use less.
"هل MongoDB هو CP ولا AP؟" ← الإجابة الصادقة إن ده حسب العملية. افتراضيًا الكتابة CP: الـ primary في الأقلية بينزل خلال حوالي 10 ثواني والكتابة بتفشل، زي التجربة دي. لكن w:1 في الفترة دي بيرجّع ack لكتابات ممكن يترجع فيها، واختبار حقيقي مع lag ضيّع الـ 500 كلهم. والقراية اختيارك: من secondary بتفضل متاحة بس ممكن قديمة، وlinearizable بترفض. و PACELC بيوصف الحياة اليومية أحسن: في الوضع العادي بقايض latency بـ consistency، حوالي 1 ms ضد 6 ms للكتابة في اختباري. فبختار لكل feature: الفلوس majority، والـ analytics ممكن أقل.

import assert from "node:assert/strict";

type ShardStats = { n: number; sum: number; top: { id: number; total: number }[] };

// الحل: كل shard بيرجع n و sum و top، مش avg جاهز
export function merge(shards: ShardStats[], k: number) {
  const n = shards.reduce((s, x) => s + x.n, 0);
  const sum = shards.reduce((s, x) => s + x.sum, 0);
  // الترتيب لازم يبقى ثابت: الـ id بيكسر التعادل عشان النتيجة متتغيرش بين طلب والتاني
  const top = shards.flatMap((x) => x.top).sort((a, b) => b.total - a.total || a.id - b.id).slice(0, k);
  return { n, avg: n === 0 ? null : sum / n, top };
}

import type pg from "pg";
import { createLab } from "./lab.ts";

// الحل: الـ resolver بيقرا سجل القرارات، ولو مفيش قرار والـ transaction قديمة بيعمل rollback
type Decision = "commit" | "abort";
export async function resolve(shard: pg.Client, decisions: Map<string, Decision>, maxAgeMs: number) {
  const prepared = await shard.query<{ gid: string; age_ms: number }>(
    `select gid, extract(epoch from now() - prepared) * 1000 as age_ms from pg_prepared_xacts where database = current_database()`,
  );
  const actions: string[] = [];
  for (const { gid, age_ms } of prepared.rows) {
    const decision = decisions.get(gid);
    // مفيش قرار لسه: ممكن الـ coordinator شغال، فبنستنى لحد الحد الأقصى
    if (!decision && age_ms < maxAgeMs) { actions.push(`wait ${gid}`); continue; }
    const sql = decision === "commit" ? "commit prepared" : "rollback prepared";
    // الـ gid جاي من pg_prepared_xacts، بس لازم يتعمله escape زي أي نص
    await shard.query(`${sql} '${gid.replaceAll("'", "''")}'`);
    actions.push(`${sql} ${gid}`);
  }
  return actions;
}

Cheat sheet · الخلاصة

  • Shard last: fix queries, scale up, replicas, cache, partition. Replicas and cache add reads only. Write throughput or data size is the real trigger.
  • You lose cross-shard joins, atomic transactions, global uniques and simple operations.
  • Key: high cardinality, no hot spots, carried by most queries. Query coverage usually beats an even spread (95% single-shard vs 10%).
  • Whales: a directory that can pin a tenant to its own shard. Time-range keys send 100% of new writes to one shard.
  • Fan-out waits for the slowest shard: 1.8% slow became 14.2% at 8 shards.
  • postgres_fdw has no 2PC: a real commit error left one shard's row behind.
  • Scatter-gather: return sum and count, never avg. Distinct needs values or a sketch. Co-locate, and use reference tables.
  • 2PC orphans hold locks, block vacuum and survive restarts. Keep it for transaction managers, and use sagas in apps.
  • Use logical shards plus a directory, moving 20% instead of 80%. A row filter's column must be in the replica identity, or UPDATE fails with 42P10.
  • Citus and Vitess move shards for you, and restrict SQL. The key and fan-outs stay your problem.
  • Postgres: synchronous_commit = on isn't read-your-writes, remote_apply is. Mongo: w:1 can lose acks, wtimeout doesn't undo. Cassandra: W+R>RF. DynamoDB: eventual by default, no strong reads on a GSI.
  • CAP is per operation: Mongo writes refuse in a minority after about 10 s, secondary reads answer. PACELC is the everyday latency trade.
بالعامية
  • الـ sharding آخر حل: صلّح الـ queries، كبّر السيرفر، replicas، cache، partition. والـ replicas والـ cache بيزوّدوا القراية بس. والكتابة أو حجم الـ data هما الدافع الحقيقي.
  • بتخسر الـ joins بين الـ shards، والـ transactions الـ atomic، والـ unique على الكل، وسهولة التشغيل.
  • الـ key: قيم كتير، ومفيش hot spots، ومعظم الـ queries شايلاه. وتغطية الـ queries غالبًا أهم من التوزيع المظبوط (95% shard واحد ضد 10%).
  • الـ whales: directory يقدر يثبت tenant على shard لوحده. والـ range على الوقت بيبعت 100% من الكتابة الجديدة لـ shard واحد.
  • الـ fan-out بيستنى أبطأ shard: 1.8% بطيئين بقوا 14.2% عند 8 shards.
  • الـ postgres_fdw مفيهوش 2PC: error حقيقي في الـ commit ساب صف على shard واحد.
  • الـ scatter-gather: رجّع sum وcount، مش avg. والـ distinct محتاج القيم أو sketch. واعمل co-location، واستخدم reference tables.
  • يتامى الـ 2PC بيمسكوا locks ويوقفوا الـ vacuum ويعيشوا بعد الـ restart. سيبه للـ transaction managers، واستخدم saga في الـ apps.
  • استخدم logical shards مع directory، بتنقل 20% بدل 80%. وعمود الـ row filter لازم يبقى في الـ replica identity، وإلا الـ UPDATE بيفشل بـ 42P10.
  • الـ Citus و Vitess بينقلوا الـ shards، وبيقيدوا الـ SQL. والـ key والـ fan-outs بيفضلوا مشكلتك.
  • Postgres: synchronous_commit = on مش read-your-writes، وremote_apply هو. و Mongo: w:1 ممكن يضيّع acks، وwtimeout مبيلغيش. و Cassandra: W+R>RF. و DynamoDB: eventual افتراضيًا، ومفيش قراءة قوية على الـ GSI.
  • الـ CAP لكل عملية: كتابة Mongo بترفض في الأقلية بعد حوالي 10 ثواني، وقراية الـ secondary بترد. و PACELC هو مقايضة الـ latency اليومية.
