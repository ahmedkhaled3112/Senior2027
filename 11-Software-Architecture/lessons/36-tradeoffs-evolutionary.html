Why this matters · ليه ده مهم

English

"It depends" is the correct answer to most architecture questions and a useless one unless you can say on what. This lesson is how you say it: turn vague wishes into scenarios you can test, measure the options, find which assumption would change the answer, and sort decisions by how hard they are to undo so your review time goes where it counts. That's the difference between a senior who has opinions and one who gets a decision through a room. All the code runs on the shared orders service and a throwaway Postgres 18; scripts go in a new l36/ folder next to after/. Timings are from a loaded Windows machine, so read them as ≈ and trust the ratios.

"حسب الموقف" هي الإجابة الصح على معظم أسئلة الـ architecture، وملهاش أي قيمة لو مش عارف تقول حسب إيه. الدرس ده هو إزاي تقولها: تحوّل الأماني المبهمة لـ scenarios تتقاس، تقيس الـ options، تلاقي أنهي افتراض لو اتغير الإجابة تتغير، وترتب القرارات حسب صعوبة الرجوع فيها عشان وقت الـ review يروح في المكان الصح. ده الفرق بين سنيور عنده آراء وسنيور بيعدّي قرار في أوضة فيها ناس. كل الكود شغال على الـ orders service المشترك و Postgres 18 مؤقت، والـ scripts بتتحط في فولدر جديد اسمه l36/ جنب after/. والأوقات من جهاز Windows عليه ضغط، فاقراها كـ ≈ وثق في النِسَب.

Part 1 — Quality attribute scenarios 🔴 Advancedالجزء 1 — الـ quality attribute scenarios

English

ATAM (the SEI's Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method) is a multi-day exercise with stakeholders. The part you can use on Tuesday is its core: name the quality attributes that matter, turn each into scenarios, evaluate each option against them, then find the sensitivity points and risks.

A scenario has four parts: stimulus (what happens), environment (under what conditions), response (what the system should do) and measure (the number that says it worked). "Payment provider down at peak: accept orders, p95 under 500 ms, charged once it returns" is a scenario. "The system must be fast" isn't: it has no stimulus, no environment and no number, so two people can agree with it and mean different things.

Keep scenarios as data, each with a function that checks it, so "does this design meet S2?" is something a script answers. A small validator rejects the vague ones before they get into a design doc.

  attributes ──▶ scenarios ──▶ options measured ──▶ sensitivity + risks
  (what matters)  (testable)    (spike numbers)      (what to verify)
                                                            │
       not a verdict: a list of what to go and check  ◀─────┘
دي خطوات الـ ATAM المبسّط من الشمال لليمين: تحدد إيه اللي يهمك، تحوله لـ scenarios تتقاس، تقيس الـ options بـ spikes، وتطلّع الـ sensitivity والـ risks. والسهم اللي راجع معناه إن الناتج مش حكم نهائي: ده قايمة بالحاجات اللي لازم تروح تتأكد منها.

// الـ validator بيرفض السيناريو اللي مش بيتقاس: كلمة زي fast من غير رقم مش requirement
const vague = /\b(fast|scalable|user-friendly|robust|highly available|performant)\b/i;
export function problemsIn(s: Partial<Scenario>): string[] {
  const problems: string[] = [];
  for (const field of ["stimulus", "environment", "response", "measure"] as const) {
    if (!s[field]) problems.push(`missing ${field}`);
  }
  if (s.measure && !/\d/.test(s.measure)) problems.push("measure has no number");
  if (vague.test(`${s.response ?? ""} ${s.measure ?? ""}`) && !/\d/.test(s.measure ?? "")) problems.push("vague words with no number");
  if (!s.check) problems.push("no way to check it");
  return problems;
}

بالعامية

الـ ATAM (الـ Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method بتاعة الـ SEI) تمرين بياخد كذا يوم مع الـ stakeholders. الجزء اللي تقدر تستخدمه يوم التلات هو القلب بتاعها: تسمّي الـ quality attributes المهمة، تحوّل كل واحدة لـ scenarios، تقيّم كل option عليها، وبعدين تطلّع الـ sensitivity points والـ risks.

الـ scenario ليه 4 أجزاء: stimulus (إيه اللي حصل)، وenvironment (تحت أي ظروف)، وresponse (النظام المفروض يعمل إيه)، وmeasure (الرقم اللي بيقول إنه نجح). "الـ payment provider واقع وقت الذروة: اقبل الأوردرات، p95 أقل من 500 ms، والـ charge يتم لما يرجع" ده scenario. "لازم النظام يبقى سريع" مش scenario: مفيهاش stimulus ولا environment ولا رقم، فاتنين ممكن يوافقوا عليها وكل واحد قاصد حاجة تانية.

خلي الـ scenarios data، وكل واحد معاه function بتفحصه، فسؤال "التصميم ده بيحقق S2؟" يبقى script هو اللي بيجاوب عليه. و validator صغير بيرفض المبهم قبل ما يدخل design doc.

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ الـ product كاتب في التذكرة "الـ checkout لازم يستحمل الضغط". بدل ما تبدأ تكتب كود، بتقعد معاه 15 دقيقة وتطلع منها: "في Black Friday بـ 300 أوردر في الدقيقة، الـ p95 أقل من 500 ms، وصفر أوردرات ضايعة لو الـ pod وقع". دلوقتي عندك حاجة تتقاس، وعندك الحجة لو قال بعدين "ليه أجّلنا feature X".

Part 2 — Evaluating options with numbers 🔴 Advancedالجزء 2 — تقييم الـ options بالأرقام

English

Three ways to take payment at checkout, written against the same schema so the comparison is fair: A charges inside the request; B writes the order and an outbox row in one transaction and charges from a worker; C does the same but reserves stock in that transaction and gives it back if the charge fails, a small saga (lesson 31).

A spike is worth more than a debate. This one runs on real Postgres with a fake provider in another process: a slow provider at 600 ms, a provider that hangs until the 2 s timeout, and a real SIGKILL while charges are in flight, then a restart.

Option A's p95 is ≈650 ms, which is the provider plus overhead, and it accepted 0 of 16 orders while the provider hung. After the crash it left 7 orders stuck in pending, and the provider had already charged all 7: money taken, no record, nothing to retry it. B and C answered in under 25 ms, accepted 16 of 16, and left nothing stuck. Their cost shows up in the last column: the retry after the crash charged some customers twice (7 and 4 orders, it varies by run), because a worker can't know whether a charge in flight went through. That's at-least-once delivery, and it's why an idempotency key on the provider call is a precondition rather than a nice-to-have (lesson 23).

// التلات options بتوع الـ checkout على نفس الـ schema، عشان المقارنة تبقى عادلة
import type pg from "pg";

export const schema = `
create table stock (sku text primary key, qty int not null check (qty >= 0));
insert into stock values ('SKU-MUG', 100000);
create table orders (id uuid primary key, status text not null);
create table outbox (order_id uuid primary key, done boolean not null default false);
`;

export type Charge = (orderId: string) => Promise<void>;

export interface CheckoutOption {
  name: string;
  // دي الدالة اللي الـ client مستني ردها: الوقت بتاعها هو الـ latency
  accept(pool: pg.Pool, charge: Charge, orderId: string): Promise<"accepted" | "failed">;
  // الـ worker بياخد order واحد من الـ outbox؛ بيرجع false لما الـ outbox يفضى
  workOne?(pool: pg.Pool, charge: Charge): Promise<boolean>;
}

// (1) الـ charge جوه الـ request: أبسط حاجة، والـ client مستني الـ provider
const syncCharge: CheckoutOption = {
  name: "A sync charge",
  async accept(pool, charge, orderId) {
    await pool.query("insert into orders values ($1, 'pending')", [orderId]);
    try {
      await charge(orderId);
    } catch {
      await pool.query("update orders set status = 'failed' where id = $1", [orderId]);
      return "failed";
    }
    await pool.query("update orders set status = 'paid' where id = $1", [orderId]);
    return "accepted";
  },
};

// الـ worker المشترك بين الـ outbox والـ saga: at-least-once، فلازم provider بـ idempotency key
const workOne = async (pool: pg.Pool, charge: Charge, onFailure?: (c: pg.PoolClient) => Promise<void>): Promise<boolean> => {
  const client = await pool.connect();
  try {
    await client.query("begin");
    const r = await client.query("select order_id from outbox where not done order by order_id limit 1 for update skip locked");
    if (r.rowCount === 0) {
      await client.query("commit");
      return false;
    }
    const id = r.rows[0].order_id as string;
    try {
      await charge(id);
    } catch {
      if (onFailure) await onFailure(client);
      await client.query("rollback");
      return true;
    }
    await client.query("update orders set status = 'paid' where id = $1", [id]);
    await client.query("update outbox set done = true where order_id = $1", [id]);
    await client.query("commit");
    return true;
  } finally {
    client.release();
  }
};

// (2) الـ request بيكتب الأوردر والـ outbox في transaction واحدة ويرد على طول
const outbox: CheckoutOption = {
  name: "B outbox + async charge",
  async accept(pool, _charge, orderId) {
    const client = await pool.connect();
    try {
      await client.query("begin");
      await client.query("insert into orders values ($1, 'accepted')", [orderId]);
      await client.query("insert into outbox (order_id) values ($1)", [orderId]);
      await client.query("commit");
      return "accepted";
    } finally {
      client.release();
    }
  },
  workOne,
};

// (3) زي الـ outbox، بس بيحجز الـ stock في نفس الـ transaction، وبيرجّعه لو الدفع فشل
const saga: CheckoutOption = {
  name: "C saga + reservation",
  async accept(pool, _charge, orderId) {
    const client = await pool.connect();
    try {
      await client.query("begin");
      const r = await client.query("update stock set qty = qty - 1 where sku = 'SKU-MUG' and qty >= 1");
      if (r.rowCount !== 1) {
        await client.query("rollback");
        return "failed";
      }
      await client.query("insert into orders values ($1, 'reserved')", [orderId]);
      await client.query("insert into outbox (order_id) values ($1)", [orderId]);
      await client.query("commit");
      return "accepted";
    } finally {
      client.release();
    }
  },
  workOne,
};

export const options: readonly CheckoutOption[] = [syncCharge, outbox, saga];

const assertSafePort = (port: unknown) => {
  // الحماية دي قبل أي connection: الـ port الفاضي معناه الخدمة الحقيقية بتاعة الجهاز
  const n = Number(port);
  if (!/^\d+$/.test(String(port)) || n < 1024 || n > 65535 || [27017, 5432, 6379].includes(n)) throw new Error("refusing port " + port);
};

const makeCharge = (url: string): Charge => async (orderId) => {
  const res = await fetch(url + "/charge", { method: "POST", body: orderId, signal: AbortSignal.timeout(2_000) });
  if (!res.ok) throw new Error("declined");
};

// الـ pool ده بيشغّل tasks بـ concurrency محدد، وبيرجع مدة كل واحدة
async function runAll(n: number, task: (i: number) => Promise<void>): Promise<number[]> {
  const times: number[] = [];
  let next = 0;
  await Promise.all(
    Array.from({ length: CONCURRENCY }, async () => {
      while (next < n) {
        const i = next++;
        const t0 = performance.now();
        await task(i);
        times.push(performance.now() - t0);
      }
    }),
  );
  return times;
}

const p95 = (xs: number[]) => [...xs].sort((a, b) => a - b)[Math.floor(xs.length * 0.95)] ?? 0;

async function drain(option: CheckoutOption, pool: pg.Pool, charge: Charge): Promise<void> {
  await Promise.all(Array.from({ length: CONCURRENCY }, async () => { while (await option.workOne?.(pool, charge)); }));
}

English

A weighted matrix turns those numbers into a score, but scores are arguments, not truth. So every weight carries its reason, and scores come from the spike wherever a spike exists; the two that are opinion say so. C wins 4.70 to 4.50 over B, and a lead that small mostly tells you where to look next.

The useful output comes from moving each weight from 0 to 100 and reporting when the winner changes. Weights that flip the result nearby are sensitivity points: here "stock truth at checkout" (C wins only from weight 4 up) and "simplicity to run" (at 25 it's a tie and past that B wins, and past 59 even A wins). In strict ATAM a sensitivity point is a property of the architecture, such as the worker's concurrency or the 2 s timeout; applying the idea to the decision's own weights is the simplified version, and it's the one you'll use most.

A trade-off point is one choice that moves two attributes in opposite directions. Going from B to C buys stock truth and costs simplicity. Then list risks and non-risks in writing. The output is a list of what to go verify, and here it's two conversations: how much does telling the truth about stock at checkout matter to the business, and can the team run compensation paths at 3 a.m.? Part 4 — Evolutionary architecture 🔴 Advancedالجزء 4 — الـ evolutionary architecture

English

Big designs up front age badly because they predict: the traffic, the team, the requirements. Evolutionary architecture designs for change instead: keep the system easy to change, change it in small deployable steps, and let fitness functions say whether each step kept its promises. Section 12 takes the same idea to system scale.

Here's a real migration: moving order emails from a synchronous call in the use case to the outbox, in three steps, each deployable and reversible. Expand: the relay runs in shadow and logs what it would send while the real email stays sync. Migrate: a flag picks who sends. Contract: delete the flag and the sync path. At every stage the 5 core tests pass, the architecture check passes and a paid order gets exactly one email.

Running both flag positions turned up the trap that a design doc never shows: flip the flag with events still undrained and you get 2 emails for 1 order, because the sync path sent one and the relay then sends one for the same event. Flip it back the same way and you get 0, because the relay in shadow mode marks the event published without sending. So the flip has a precondition: drain the outbox first, in both directions. Reversible never means free.

  back: nothing      back: drop shadow   back: drain, flip     back: git revert

Why big-bang migrations fail more often One big PR means one big rollback, and a rollback after a data change is usually not one. Small steps keep the blast radius of each mistake to one step, and you find out about the 2-email problem in stage 2 instead of on launch day with every order.

Part 5 — One-way and two-way doors 🔴 Advancedالجزء 5 — الـ one-way والـ two-way doors

English

Jeff Bezos's 2016 letter to shareholders says: "Many decisions are reversible, two-way doors. Those decisions can use a light-weight process." The other half is that irreversible ones deserve the slow, careful process. Most teams get this backwards: three weeks on the framework, an afternoon on the public ID format.

You can score it. Reversal cost has three parts: how many files depend on the choice (measured from the code), how much data must migrate, and how many parties outside the repo depend on it. Data and external consumers weigh ten times a code file, a choice you should write down and argue about. Express comes out at 2: it's behind presentation/http.ts, and lesson 13 really swapped it for Fastify by changing 2 files. The public order ID comes out at 61, because it's in customers' emails, bookmarks and partner integrations.

You can also turn a one-way door into a two-way door: an abstraction (the PaymentGateway port makes the provider cheap to change), a flag (Part 4), or expand/contract on data and contracts (add public_id, accept both, migrate, drop the old one). A port doesn't make the database decision two-way, though: it helps the code and the data move stays expensive, which is what the score says. Spend your time on the one-way ones; for the rest, let the author decide and move on. A two-way door is also the shortest test for whether something needs an ADR.

Part 6 — The last responsible moment 🔴 Advancedالجزء 6 — آخر لحظة مسؤولة

English

Delay a decision until the cost of not deciding exceeds the cost of deciding. Waiting buys information, and for reversible decisions information is usually worth more than time. Bezos's letter puts a number on it: most decisions should be made with "somewhere around 70% of the information you wish you had".

A small calculator makes the trade concrete. Deciding now costs the build plus the chance you're wrong times the rework. Deferring costs the delay plus the build, once, with better information. Picking Kafka today comes out at 10.0 weeks; BullMQ behind a port for 6 months at 0.1 weeks of pain a month comes out at 6.6, and deferring wins whenever the chance the need changes is above 0.08. The public ID comes out the other way: 3.4 against 13.0, because every order created before you decide makes the later migration bigger. It's an argument in numbers, so test it with your own.

"Last responsible moment" is also the most popular excuse for not deciding. A real deferral has a written trigger and an owner. If it has been deferred twice, or if work is already blocked waiting for it, you've passed the responsible moment. The fix is an ADR that records the decision to wait along with the trigger that ends the wait.

الناتج: Kafka النهارده 10 أسابيع، والتأجيل 6.6، والتأجيل بيكسب لو احتمال تغيّر الاحتياج فوق 0.08 (وهو 0.5) فـ "أجّل واكتب الـ trigger". الـ public ID: دلوقتي 3.4 قصاد 13 أسبوع تأجيل، فـ "قرر دلوقتي". وآخر سطرين: تأجيل الـ broker حقيقي لأن معاه trigger وصاحب، وتأجيل الـ tenant isolation عذر: مفيش trigger ولا صاحب، اتأجل 3 مرات، والشغل واقف عليه.
# ADR-0012: Start on BullMQ behind a QueuePort, decide on Kafka later

Status: accepted
Context: 40 jobs/s today, one consumer. Kafka costs about 6 weeks to adopt and run.
Decision: BullMQ on Redis behind QueuePort. Business code imports only the port.
Consequences: no replay, no fan-out to many consumers.
Trigger to revisit: sustained 2,000 jobs/s, or a second consumer needs replay.
Owner: platform team. Review date: 2027-05-01.
دي الـ ADR اللي بتحوّل التأجيل لقرار: بدأنا بـ BullMQ ورا port، والسياق 40 job في الثانية و consumer واحد، والتكلفة إن مفيش replay. وأهم سطرين: الـ trigger (2,000 job في الثانية باستمرار، أو consumer تاني محتاج replay)، والمالك مع تاريخ مراجعة.
بالعامية

أجّل القرار لحد ما تكلفة عدم القرار تبقى أكبر من تكلفة القرار. الانتظار بيشتري معلومة، وفي القرارات اللي بترجع فيها المعلومة غالبًا أهم من الوقت. وجواب Bezos بيحط رقم: معظم القرارات تتاخد بحوالي 70% من المعلومات اللي نفسك تكون عندك.

حاسبة صغيرة بتخلي الموضوع ملموس. القرار دلوقتي تكلفته البناء زائد احتمال إنك غلطان مضروب في إعادة الشغل. والتأجيل تكلفته الانتظار زائد البناء مرة واحدة بمعلومة أحسن. اختيار Kafka النهارده طلع 10.0 أسابيع؛ و BullMQ ورا port لمدة 6 شهور بـ 0.1 أسبوع معاناة في الشهر طلع 6.6، والتأجيل بيكسب لو احتمال تغيّر الاحتياج فوق 0.08. والـ public ID طلع بالعكس: 3.4 قصاد 13.0، لأن كل أوردر بيتعمل قبل ما تقرر بيكبّر الـ migration بعدين. ده كلام بالأرقام، فجرّبه بأرقامك إنت.

"آخر لحظة مسؤولة" هي كمان أشهر عذر لعدم القرار. التأجيل الحقيقي ليه trigger مكتوب وصاحب. لو اتأجل مرتين، أو الشغل واقف عليه، يبقى عدّيت اللحظة المسؤولة. والحل ADR بيسجل قرار الانتظار ومعاه الـ trigger اللي بينهيه.

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ لما حد يقول "لازم Kafka عشان هنكبر"، اسأل: "إيه الـ trigger؟". لو الإجابة أرقام، اكتبها في ADR وابدأ بـ BullMQ ورا port (الدرس 13 شغّل BullMQ حقيقي كـ driver). ولو الموضوع اتأجل تلات مرات في الـ planning من غير حد مسؤول، اقفله: حد ياخد ساعة ويقرر.
Interview question "Isn't 'defer the decision' just procrastination?" → Only without a trigger and an owner. I write down what would force the decision and who watches for it, and I hide the choice behind a port so waiting is cheap. If it blocks work or has slipped twice, I stop deferring. For one-way doors like a public ID, waiting costs more, so I decide early.
"التأجيل مش مجرد تسويف؟" ← بيبقى تسويف بس لو مفيش trigger ولا صاحب. بكتب إيه اللي هيجبرنا نقرر ومين بيراقبه، وبخبّي الاختيار ورا port عشان الانتظار يبقى رخيص. ولو واقف عليه شغل أو اتأجل مرتين، بوقف تأجيل. وفي الـ one-way doors زي الـ public ID الانتظار تكلفته أكبر، فبقرر بدري.

❌ A wish, not a scenario

Cheat sheet · الخلاصة

  • Scenario = stimulus, environment, response, measure, plus a way to check it.
  • Spike the 2 or 3 scenarios that matter; a half-day number beats a week of debate.
  • Sync charge: ≈650 ms p95, 0 of 16 accepted when the provider hung, 7 orders charged but stuck after a crash.
  • Outbox or saga: under 25 ms, 16 of 16, 0 stuck, but retries charge twice unless the provider call has an idempotency key.
  • Scores are arguments: every weight has a written why, and opinions are labelled.
  • Vary the weights and report what flips the winner. The output is a list to verify, not a verdict.
  • Trade-off point: one choice moves two attributes in opposite directions.
  • Migrate in deployable steps: expand, migrate behind a flag, contract. Tests and fitness green at each.
  • Drain in-flight work before flipping a flag, in both directions: without it, 2 emails one way and 0 the other.
  • Reversal cost = code files + data + external consumers. Spend review time on the one-way doors only.
  • Abstraction, flags and expand/contract turn one-way doors into two-way, though a port won't cheapen a data migration.
  • Defer with a written trigger and an owner. No trigger, or deferred twice, means it's an excuse.

  • الـ scenario = stimulus و environment و response و measure، ومعاهم طريقة فحص.
  • اعمل spike لأهم 2 أو 3 scenarios؛ رقم من نص يوم أحسن من أسبوع نقاش.
  • الـ sync charge: p95 حوالي 650 ms، و 0 من 16 اتقبلوا والـ provider معلّق، و 7 أوردرات اتخصموا وفضلوا معلّقين بعد الـ crash.
  • الـ outbox أو الـ saga: أقل من 25 ms، و 16 من 16، وصفر معلّق، بس الـ retries بتحاسب مرتين لو نداء الـ provider مفيهوش idempotency key.
  • الدرجات حجج: كل وزن معاه سببه مكتوب، والآراء مكتوب عليها إنها آراء.
  • حرّك الأوزان وقول إيه اللي بيقلب الفايز. الناتج قايمة تتأكد منها مش حكم.
  • الـ trade-off point: اختيار واحد بيحرك attribute-ين في اتجاهين عكس بعض.
  • انقل بخطوات deployable: expand، وmigrate ورا flag، وcontract. والـ tests والـ fitness خضر في كل خطوة.
  • فضّي الشغل اللي في الطريق قبل قلب الـ flag في الاتجاهين: من غير كده 2 إيميل في اتجاه و 0 في التاني.

  • الـ abstraction والـ flags والـ expand/contract بيحولوا الـ one-way لـ two-way، بس الـ port مش هيرخّص نقل الـ data.
