Why this matters · ليه ده مهم

English

Most changes worth making need people who don't report to you to agree. You can get your way by being louder or by outlasting everyone, and it costs you the next ten decisions. Will Larson describes that person as often right, "but right in a way that sucks the oxygen out of the room" (Learn to never be wrong). This lesson is about the quieter routes: evidence, one-to-ones before the meeting, knowing who decides, losing without sulking, choosing what to fight over, and where credit and blame go. In interviews it's "tell me about convincing a team that didn't want your proposal."

Treat this lesson as advice. The Orders team, the names and every number in the scenarios are invented for teaching, and the mid-versus-senior contrasts are teaching patterns, not measurements. The team sits at a mid-size online store: Mona (product manager), Hany (engineering manager), Karim (mid-level), Salma (junior), Nour (infra), Yasmin (support lead), and you.

بالعامية

Will Larson بيوصف الشخص ده إنه غالبًا صح، "بس صح بطريقة بتسحب الأكسجين من الأوضة". الدرس ده عن الطرق الأهدى: الدليل، والـ one-to-ones قبل الاجتماع، وتعرف مين بيقرر، وتخسر من غير ما تزعل وتعطّل، وتختار تحارب في إيه، والـ credit واللوم بيروحوا فين. وفي الـ interviews بييجي في صورة "احكيلي إزاي أقنعت فريق مكانش عايز اقتراحك".

Part 1 — Evidence beats volume 🔴 Advancedالجزء 1 — الدليل أقوى من الصوت العالي

English

The situation. POST /orders and the payment-webhook handler need a store for idempotency keys. The thread has been going for two days. Karim says Redis, because it's faster. Nour doesn't want another dependency on the write path. You have an opinion too.

What a mid-level engineer does. Argues louder in the thread and restates the same point with more adjectives. The thread stops being about the store and becomes about who's right.

What a senior does. Stops writing opinions and spends a day finding out: "I tried both in a branch. Under our peak load Postgres cost 3 ms more than Redis at p95, and we skip a dependency. Numbers and script are here." How to get honest numbers is in Profiling & Load Testing and Do you need Kafka?

Why the second works. A measurement moves the argument from people to a result anyone can rerun. Publishing the script invites people to attack the method instead of you. And saying what would change your mind lets someone agree with you without losing.

**Idempotency keys for POST /orders: I tried both. Recommendation: Postgres, in the same transaction as the order.**

Lesson note: every number in this message is invented for this page.

How I tested: branch spike/idempotency-store, staging, production schema, 60 requests/second for 10 minutes, which is last year's busiest hour times 1.5. Script and raw output are in scripts/load/idempotency.md.

                     p95      p99      errors   new dependency
Today, no key store  142 ms   210 ms   0        -
Redis, SET NX        146 ms   224 ms   0        yes, on the write path
Postgres, same tx    149 ms   231 ms   0        none

What I read from this: Postgres costs 3 ms more than Redis at p95, on a request that takes 140 ms. Nobody will feel that. In exchange the key and the order commit together, so a crash can't leave one without the other, and the write path doesn't need Redis to be up.

What this doesn't show: 10x load. I didn't test that because next year's plan is 2x.

What would change my mind: a p95 budget under 100 ms on this endpoint, or sustained traffic over about 400 requests/second.

Karim, you made the case for Redis and you know that path better than I do. Can you try to break the test?

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ لما تلاقي نقاش في PR أو channel لف 3 مرات حوالين نفس النقطة (REST ولا GraphQL، index ده ينفع ولا لأ، cache هنا ولا لأ)، بطّل تكتب واعمل تجربة صغيرة: EXPLAIN ANALYZE على data قريبة من production، أو load test ساعة على branch. وابعت النتيجة ومعاها "إيه اللي يغيّر رأيي".
Trade-off: a prototype can win an argument about the wrong question The Redis-versus-Postgres numbers say nothing about whether idempotency belongs in this service at all, or who gets paged when the store is down. A working branch also creates momentum ("it's already built") and can hide the hard part. Before you build, write one sentence: "This test decides X, and a result of Y would change my recommendation."
الـ trade-off: الـ prototype ممكن يكسب نقاش عن السؤال الغلط. أرقام Redis ولا Postgres مبتقولش حاجة عن هل الـ idempotency أصلًا مكانها في الـ service ده، ولا مين هيتصحى لما الـ store يقع. والـ branch الشغال كمان بيعمل زخم ("ما هو اتبنى خلاص")، وممكن يخبّي الجزء الصعب. قبل ما تبني اكتب جملة واحدة: "التجربة دي بتحسم X، ولو طلعت Y هغيّر رأيي".

Part 2 — Agreement before the meeting 🔴 Advancedالجزء 2 — الاتفاق قبل الاجتماع

English

The situation. You propose moving invoice-pdf and notifications to the dedicated Redis, and you first show it in the architecture sync. Nour asks about the backup window, Karim about in-flight jobs, and Hany asks whether support knows. Forty minutes later: "let's take this offline."

What a mid-level engineer does. Shows it for the first time in the meeting. Everyone sees it cold, objections land in public, and people end up defending positions they took out loud.

What a senior does. Talks one-to-one with the people it touches first, hears the objections, changes the proposal, and uses the meeting to confirm it. The Japanese word for this is nemawashi, which the Lean Enterprise Institute defines as "gaining acceptance and preapproval for a proposal" with stakeholders, with formal approval coming in a meeting afterwards. The term literally means preparing the ground for planting.

Why the second works. In a one-to-one, people can say "this worries me" or "I don't get this bit" without losing face, and the proposal really does get better. Larson's advice for tense discussions applies here: "ask three good questions before you share your perspective." He also names the cost and the payoff: "It feels like it's slow because it can take longer to get started, but ultimately it's fast because you're more likely to complete the work without disruption." If nothing changed after your one-to-ones, you weren't listening, you were lobbying.

Part 3 — Who actually decides 🔴 Advancedالجزء 3 — مين اللي بيقرر فعلًا

English

The situation. The dedicated-Redis proposal needs Hany's agreement on paper. But Nour's objection is the real obstacle, since Nour will run it, and support will carry the cost during the cutover. You've spent a week on a detailed deck for Hany.

What a mid-level engineer does. Pitches to the wrong person in the wrong language: BullMQ lock details for Hany, a business case for Nour. Or treats Hany's yes as the finish line, and support finds out from angry customers.

What a senior does. Makes a small private map: for each person, whether they decide, are affected or are consulted, what they care about, and how to talk to them. Then goes to each in their own language, and tells Hany early that it's coming. Larson's first rule for working with a manager is "Never surprise your manager" (Staying aligned with authority).

Why the second works. Formal authority and the practical ability to stop something aren't the same. Someone with no vote on paper can sink a plan by being right about a risk, or by being the person who'd get paged. And people carrying the cost should hear about it from you first, which also tends to get you better plans.

English

The situation. You have five live disagreements this sprint: tabs in a config file, a new ORM, lock timeouts on deploys, a naming scheme for queues, and retries on payments.

English

Try it yourself · جرّب بنفسك

1. Name someone, with specifics 🟢 easy This week, credit a colleague publicly: what they did, why it was hard, and what changed because of it. Ask them first if they're OK with it. Write it by hand, not with AI. If you can't do this at work, thank a maintainer of a library you use with the same level of detail.
الأسبوع ده ادّي credit لزميل قدام الناس: عمل إيه، وليه كان صعب، وإيه اللي اتغير بسببه. اسأله الأول لو موافق. واكتبها بإيدك مش بالـ AI. ولو مش هينفع في الشغل، اشكر maintainer لمكتبة بتستخدمها بنفس درجة التفصيل.
Hint · تلميح Avoid "great work." Fill in this sentence instead: "X did Y, which was hard because Z, and now W."
ابعد عن "شغل عظيم". املا الجملة دي بدلها: "فلان عمل كذا، وده كان صعب عشان كذا، ودلوقتي بقى كذا".
Solution · الحل Worked example: Karim writes "Credit to Yasmin. She spotted that the refund tickets all came from one coupon code, which took us from 'random bug' to the right query in an hour. The fix went out the same day." She'd said yes first, in a direct message.
مثال: كريم كتب "الـ credit لياسمين. هي اللي لاحظت إن كل tickets الـ refund جاية من coupon code واحد، وده نقلنا من 'bug عشوائي' للـ query الصح في ساعة. والحل نزل في نفس اليوم." وكان سألها الأول في رسالة وقالت آه.
2. Draw your map, privately 🟡 medium Take a live proposal and make a private map of who decides, who's affected and who's consulted, with what each person cares about and how to talk to them. Write it by hand, not with AI, and keep it in your own notes. If there's no live proposal at work, do this for a public RFC in an open-source project you follow.
خد اقتراح شغال حاليًا واعمل خريطة private: مين بيقرر، مين متأثر، مين بيتاخد رأيه، وكل واحد بيهمه إيه وتكلمه إزاي. اكتبها بإيدك مش بالـ AI، وخليها في النوتس بتاعتك. ولو مفيش اقتراح شغال في الشغل، اعملها على RFC عام في مشروع open source بتتابعه.
Hint · تلميح Ask two questions for each row: who gets paged if this goes wrong, and who hears about it first from a customer?
اسأل سؤالين لكل صف: مين هيتصحى لو الموضوع باظ؟ ومين هيسمع عنه الأول من عميل؟
Solution · الحل Worked example: Karim maps a coupon-engine rewrite. Mona decides the scope, and he'd assumed Hany did. Salma would do most of the tests, and he hadn't planned to talk to her. Yasmin's agents are the ones who explain failed coupons to customers. He ends up talking to Salma and Yasmin first.
مثال: كريم عمل خريطة لإعادة كتابة الـ coupon engine. منى هي اللي بتقرر الـ scope، وهو كان فاكر هاني. سلمى هتعمل معظم الـ tests، وماكانش ناوي يكلمها. والـ agents بتوع ياسمين هما اللي بيشرحوا فشل الـ coupons للعملاء. فبدأ بسلمى وياسمين الأول.
3. Change something before the meeting 🔴 at work Before your next proposal goes to a meeting, hold at least two one-to-ones and change something because of them. Say so in the room, by name. Do the writing yourself, not with AI. If you have no proposal at work, discuss an idea in an open-source project's issue tracker before opening the PR.
قبل ما اقتراحك الجاي يدخل اجتماع، اعمل على الأقل 2 one-to-one وغيّر حاجة بسببهم. وقول كده في الاجتماع بالاسم. اكتب بنفسك مش بالـ AI. ولو مفيش اقتراح في الشغل، ناقش فكرة في issues بتاعة مشروع open source قبل ما تفتح الـ PR.
Hint · تلميح Ask for what worries them, not for support: "What's the first thing that would go wrong here?"
اسأل عن اللي قالقهم مش عن تأييدهم: "إيه أول حاجة ممكن تبوظ هنا؟"
Solution · الحل Worked example: before proposing that the notifications worker move to its own deployment, Salma asks Nour and Yasmin what worries them. Nour asks for a memory limit per worker, and Yasmin asks that SMS failures show up in the support dashboard. Both go into the proposal, and in the meeting Salma opens with "Nour and Yasmin changed two things in this."
مثال: قبل ما تقترح نقل worker الـ notifications لـ deployment لوحده، سلمى سألت نور وياسمين إيه اللي قالقهم. نور طلب memory limit لكل worker، وياسمين طلبت إن فشل الـ SMS يظهر في dashboard الـ support. الاتنين اتحطوا في الاقتراح، وفي الاجتماع سلمى فتحت بـ "نور وياسمين غيّروا حاجتين هنا".

Cheat sheet · الخلاصة

  • Two days of thread? Stop writing, run the test, post the script, say what would change your mind.
  • Before building a prototype: "This test decides X, and a result of Y would change my recommendation."
  • Nemawashi: one-to-ones first, change the proposal, then the meeting confirms it.
  • If nothing changed after the one-to-ones, you weren't listening.
  • Be open: "I've talked to Nour and Karim about this already, and here's what changed."
  • Map who decides, who's affected and who can really stop it. Keep the map private. Never surprise your manager.
  • Rejected? Irreversible, safety or customer money: insist once, in writing. Otherwise commit.
  • Commit means no "I told you so", no slow-walking, and defending it to your team.
  • "Disagree and commit" requires the disagreeing to happen first.
  • Sort battles by cost of being wrong and difficulty of undoing. Letting go has to be real.
  • Credit by name, with specifics, after asking. Tell their manager too.
  • Failure: "That was my call." Fix, what changes. Don't name who ran the command.

  • قبل ما تبني prototype: "التجربة دي بتحسم X، ولو طلعت Y هغيّر اقتراحي".
  • Nemawashi: الـ one-to-ones الأول، غيّر الاقتراح، وبعدين الاجتماع يأكد.
  • لو مفيش حاجة اتغيرت بعد الـ one-to-ones، يبقى ماكنتش بتسمع.
  • خليك صريح: "أنا اتكلمت مع نور وكريم قبل كده، وده اللي اتغير".
