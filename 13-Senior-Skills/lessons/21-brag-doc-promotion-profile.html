Why this matters · ليه ده مهم

English

Everything in this section so far produced something real: an RFC, a postmortem, a junior who got better. This last lesson makes that work checkable by someone who wasn't there. Three moments need it: a performance review, a promotion decision, and the interview question "what's your biggest achievement?". In each one, a person has minutes to judge months of work, and memory is a poor witness. As Julia Evans puts it, you don't remember everything you did, and your manager doesn't either.

The running example is Karim, the mid-level engineer on the Orders team at a mid-size online store, with Hany as his manager, Mona as product manager, Salma as the junior, Nour on infra and Yasmin leading support. The team, names and numbers in the scenarios are invented for teaching. Treat everything here about promotion as advice: how it works varies by company, and the Mid-versus-Senior contrasts are teaching patterns, not measured facts.

بالعامية

كل اللي فات في القسم ده طلّع حاجات حقيقية: RFC، postmortem، جونيور بقى أحسن. الدرس الأخير ده بيخلّي الشغل ده حد مكانش موجود يقدر يتأكد منه. فيه 3 لحظات محتاجة كده: الـ performance review، قرار الترقية، وسؤال الـ interview "إيه أكبر إنجاز ليك؟". في كل واحدة منهم، حد عنده دقايق يحكم على شهور شغل، والذاكرة شاهد ضعيف. وزي ما Julia Evans بتقول: إنت مش فاكر كل اللي عملته، والـ manager بتاعك كمان مش فاكر.

المثال اللي ماشي معانا هو كريم، الـ mid-level في فريق Orders في online store متوسط الحجم. هاني الـ manager بتاعه، منى الـ product manager، سلمى الجونيور، نور في الـ infra، وياسمين بتقود الـ support. الفريق والأسماء والأرقام اللي في السيناريوهات كلها متألفة عشان الشرح. وأي كلام هنا عن الترقية خده على إنه نصيحة: الترقية بتختلف من شركة للتانية، والمقارنات بين الـ Mid والـ Senior أنماط للتعليم، مش حقايق متقاسة.

Part 1 — The brag document 🔴 Advancedالجزء 1 — الـ brag document

English

The situation. The October review cycle opens and Hany asks Karim for a summary of his year. Karim can remember the last six weeks. March is a blank, even though March is when the webhook inbox shipped.

What a mid-level engineer does. Rebuilds the year from memory in one evening, using PR titles and chat search, and ends up with "Worked on payments. Helped the team. Fixed bugs." Hany can't use that line, because nothing in it can be checked or repeated to anyone else.

What a senior does. Keeps one running document and adds an entry every month, which is what README artifact 8 asks for. Julia Evans, whose post is the usual reference for this, reports that many people update theirs every two weeks and that she prefers one long session every six months or a year, so try what works and keep the habit you'll really keep. The categories are hers: goals, projects, collaboration and mentorship, design and documentation, company building, what you learned, outside work, and the fuzzy work that's hard to count. Each line says what you did, a number if one exists, who benefited, and a link.

Why the second works. Evans's argument is that your manager, however good, can't advocate for work they don't know about. A dated line with a number can go straight into a review. Fuzzy work gets written down too, and she singles it out as the work "most likely to fall under the radar".

Accurate means neither inflated nor understated. In her words: "you don't have to try to make your work sound better than it is. Just make it sound exactly as good as it is!" So say who did what. Karim wrote the RFC and ran the rollout, Salma owned the worker, and the entry says both. Come back later and add what really happened after launch, which is why the March entry below has a line dated 30 April.

# Brag document: Karim, Orders team

Goals this year: reach Senior by the October cycle. Lead one incident. Own one feature end to end.

## March

### Projects
- Moved payment webhooks to an inbox table (RFC-12).
  - My part: wrote the RFC, designed the table and the dedupe key, ran the rollout.
  - Others: Salma owned the worker. Nour checked the Postgres load.
  - Impact: duplicate charges went from about 9 a month (Jan-Feb, counted from support tickets) to 0.
  - Who benefited: customers no longer charged twice. Yasmin's team has about 9 fewer refund cases a month.
  - Result check, added 30 April: still 0 after 6 weeks. One late webhook was replayed by hand, no customer impact.

### Collaboration and mentorship
- Started a weekly pairing hour with Salma. This month she asked what EXPLAIN meant, and we read two real plans together.

### Design and documentation
- Wrote ADR-007 from RFC-12.
- Wrote the inbox replay runbook after Yasmin asked how a stuck payment gets fixed.

### Company building
- Updated the onboarding page so a new hire can run the webhook flow locally.

### What I learned
- Partial unique indexes. How our provider's retry schedule behaves in practice.

### Outside work
- Nothing this month.

### Fuzzy work
- Goal: reviews on payment code should catch duplicate-processing bugs.
- Did: asked "what happens if this runs twice?" on 4 PRs.
- Effect, small so far: Salma asked the same question herself on PR 431.

Part 2 — Finding the real bar 🔴 Advancedالجزء 2 — تعرف المعيار الحقيقي

English

The situation. Karim has been mid-level for three years and tells his friends he's "due". Nobody at the company has ever told him what Senior means there.

What a mid-level engineer does. Assumes Senior means more years or harder tickets, grabs the hardest ticket on the board, and waits.

What a senior does. Finds out what the bar is. He asks for the leveling guide. There isn't one, so he asks Hany what he'd expect from a Senior on this team, writes it down with what would show each item, and gets Hany to correct and agree the text. He also asks the process questions: who decides, when, and whether there's a limit on how many.

If your company has no ladder, a public one gives you words to start from. Dropbox publishes its Engineering Career Framework. Treat it as an example, not a standard: its own overview says it "is not a promotion checklist" and tells you to meet your manager to agree expectations. Its software ladder runs IC1 to IC4 "Software Engineer" and then IC5 "Staff", with no rung called Senior, so read IC3 and IC4 and decide with your manager which is closer to what Senior means where you work.

Why the second works. You can't aim at a bar you've never read. An unwritten bar can also move without anyone noticing, while a written list turns "you need more maturity" into items you can point at. Section 14 · 3.5 covers the interview-style side of an internal promotion.

# Senior expectations, Orders team

Drafted by Karim. Corrected and agreed by Hany on 28 January.
There is no written ladder here, so this is what Hany would look for.
It is not a promise of promotion. Dropbox's public framework (IC3, IC4) was used only for wording.

E1 Scope: takes a vague problem to a design doc and milestones without someone breaking it down.
   Would show it: one project from vague ask to shipped.
E2 Ownership: owns a feature past the merge, including metrics, bugs and the week-one result.
   Would show it: a ship note with a day-7 result.
E3 Production: leads an incident calmly and runs the postmortem to closed actions.
   Would show it: one incident led, one postmortem, actions closed.
E4 Judgement: can explain the trade-offs behind decisions in our system, in writing and out loud.
   Would show it: one RFC decided and built.
E5 Review: reviews catch design, correctness and security problems, including outside his own area.
   Would show it: 5 review threads that Hany picks at random.
E6 Growing others: someone on the team is clearly better because of his help.
   Would show it: a dated log, and that person agrees.
E7 Product: Mona asks his view before she decides, not after.
   Would show it: Mona's own words.
E8 Cross-team: gets agreement on work that depends on another team, with the contract in writing.
   Would show it: one agreed interface or contract.

Not part of the bar: years at the company, tickets closed, hours worked.
Not expected at this level: owning the roadmap, hiring decisions.

Process
- Who decides: Hany proposes, the head of engineering decides.
- When: April and October.
- Limits: Hany will find out whether a slot is needed this year and tell Karim by 15 February.

Part 3 — The promotion packet 🔴 Advancedالجزء 3 — الـ promotion packet

English

The situation. It's 30 June, month six of Karim's nine. In October, Hany will argue for him in a room Karim won't be in. This is README artifact 11.

What a mid-level engineer does. Has no document. He either expects Hany to remember, or writes a list of tickets the night before the deadline.

What a senior does. Started the packet in January, before there was much to put in it, and uses it as a map. That's Will Larson's advice in his guide to promotion packets: start "long before" you expect the promotion. His guide is written for Staff-plus, and adapting it to Senior is this lesson's choice, not his: at Senior the scope is usually one team rather than an organization. His sections are projects with impact, organizational improvements, quantifiable impact, mentorship, glue work, advocates, and gaps with one sentence on each. Karim adds expectation IDs from part 2, so every claim maps to the bar, and a Production section because this team's bar has one.

Larson's order of work is worth copying: know why you want this; expect it to take time (he says promotions at Staff level are built over quarters, halves and years); take your manager the empty packet early; write a first version in about an hour; wait two days and edit; ask trusted peers to read it; ask your manager to read it with a focus on gaps; then keep reviewing it together, especially if your manager changes. Your company may have its own format, and you can translate into it at the end.

Why the second works. Hany can lift text straight into his case. Missing evidence shows up in June, when Karim can still do something about it, and Karim is the one who names his gaps first.

# Promotion packet: Karim, Mid to Senior

Part 4 — The conversation 🔴 Advancedالجزء 4 — الكلام مع الـ manager

English

The situation. It's January. Karim wants Senior, and Hany doesn't know, because Karim has never said so.

What a mid-level engineer does. Waits to be noticed. He works harder, hopes, and gets more frustrated every quarter.

What a senior does. Says it, in writing, with a proposal attached: "I want to reach Senior in about 9 months. Here's what I think the bar is and where I stand. What gaps do you see?" After the meeting he sends notes, so there's one record of what was said. Then he spends 15 minutes against the list in the first 1:1 of every month: what moved, what didn't, and what he needs from Hany.

Why the second works. A manager can't back a goal they don't know you have. Larson's advice is to tell your manager directly and bring the empty packet, so that they know you're interested and can guide you. Written notes protect both sides when memories differ or when Hany leaves, and Larson points out that the packet helps limit the progress that's often lost when a manager changes. The monthly check means a no in October can't be a surprise.

If it isn't coming despite the evidence. Find out whether the blocker is you, budget or timing, and follow the tree below. Section 14 · 3.5 covers internal promotion and what to do next. Don't threaten: an ultimatum turns the person who has to argue for you into an opponent, and then you'd have to act on it.

Cheat sheet · الخلاصة

  • Brag document: one running file, a dated entry each month (README artifact 8). Each line: what you did, a number, who benefited, a link.
  • Evans's categories: goals, projects, collaboration and mentorship, design and docs, company building, learning, outside work, fuzzy work.
  • Accurate means neither inflated nor understated. Say who did what, and come back later to add the result.
  • Find the bar: read the ladder, or ask your manager, write it down and get it agreed. Dropbox's framework is an example, not a standard. Ask who decides and when.
  • Packet: start early and use it as a map. Larson's sections (written for Staff-plus, adapted here): projects, organizational improvements, numbers, mentorship, glue work, advocates, gaps with one sentence each.
  • Name a mentee only with their consent, and never claim their work.
  • The ask: "I want to reach Senior in about 9 months. Here's what I think the bar is and where I stand. What gaps do you see?" Send notes the same day, and check the list every month.
  • After a no: specific gaps? fair? you or budget? can you show it here? If not, look outside quietly. Never threaten.
  • Profile: only what you can discuss for ten minutes. Public links are anonymised and approved, never the internal doc.
  • Exit test and 11 artifacts: ✅🔄❌ with links, read by someone who might disagree.
  • All of this is advice. How promotion works varies by company.
