Why this matters · ليه ده مهم

English

Lesson 04 covered reviewing well as an individual. This lesson is about what a review does to the team. A reviewer who asks "what happens if this job runs twice?" prevents a double refund, and also hands the author a question they'll ask themselves next time. A study of review at Microsoft (Bacchelli & Bird, ICSE 2013) found that finding defects was the main reason people gave for reviewing, but that reviews turned out to be less about defects than expected, and also delivered knowledge transfer, team awareness and alternative solutions. So your comments are one of the main ways a team learns.

Treat this as advice: if you interview for senior roles, expect judgement questions on exactly this, such as what you do with a huge PR whose design is wrong.

One note before we start. The Orders team at a mid-size online store, its people (Mona the product manager, Hany the engineering manager, Karim a mid-level engineer, Salma a junior, Nour on infra, Yasmin leading support) and every number in the scenarios are invented for teaching. "You" is the reviewer. "Mid" and "senior" are shorthand for two habits, not measured differences between people with those titles.

Part 1 — What you're reading for 🟡 Intermediateالجزء 1 — إنت بتقرا عشان تدوّر على إيه

English

The situation. Karim opens a PR that adds a refund-order BullMQ job. It calls the payment provider, then sets refunded_at on the order, with attempts: 5 for transient failures. The diff is tidy and the tests are green.

What a mid-level engineer does. Leaves nine comments about naming: refundAmt, cfg, a file that should be kebab-case. All of them are reasonable. Karim renames things, the PR merges, and nobody has noticed that the job isn't idempotent.

What a senior does. Reads in a fixed order, most expensive first: design (should this exist, and is it in the right place), correctness and edge cases, security, performance, tests. Then writes the one question that matters: "What happens if this job runs twice? The provider would refund twice." BullMQ delivery is at-least-once: a worker that dies after the provider accepted the refund, or a timeout on a call that actually succeeded, runs the job again. The fix is an idempotency key the provider sees, and that only gets built if someone asks.

Why it works. Reviewer attention is the scarce resource, and every minute spent on names comes out of the things no tool will catch. Google's reviewer guide starts in the same place: "The most important thing to cover in a review is the overall design of the CL." Style comes near the end of its list. Its standard is to approve once a change "definitely improves the overall code health", even if it isn't perfect. The order below is this lesson's recommendation, close to Google's but not a universal rule.

Names still matter when they mislead: an amount that could be EGP or piastres is a correctness comment, not a style one. For design smells worth naming in a review, see Anti-Patterns: Catching Them in Code Review.

## Review: #1482 add `refund-order` job

Thanks Karim, the job is easy to follow. I stopped at three things that matter more than the rest.

**issue (blocking): What happens if this job runs twice?**
`process()` calls `provider.refund()` and only then writes `refunded_at`. BullMQ delivery is at-least-once and this job has `attempts: 5`, so a timeout on a call that actually succeeded runs it again. The provider would refund twice: on a 1,250 EGP order, that's 2,500 EGP back to the customer.
Could we create a `refunds` row first and send its id as the provider's idempotency key, so a retry reuses it?

**question (blocking): Who is allowed to trigger this?**
`POST /admin/orders/:id/refund` only checks `isAuthenticated`, and today that includes every support login. Is that intended? I'd expect a `refunds:write` permission and a check that the amount doesn't exceed what was captured minus what's already been refunded.

**suggestion (non-blocking): Test the failure, not just the success.**
The test mocks `provider.refund()` and asserts one call. Could we run the handler twice with the same job data and assert one provider call and one `refunds` row? That's the test that would have caught the first comment.

I didn't comment on names or import order. If we care about those, let's add the ESLint rule rather than spend reviews on them. I'll open that PR.

Part 2 — Asking instead of ordering 🟡 Intermediateالجزء 2 — تسأل بدل ما تأمر

English

The situation. The invoice-pdf worker adds up lines with reduce and no initial value. On an order where every line was refunded, lines is empty, so the call throws, the job fails on every retry, and the customer never gets a document.

What a mid-level engineer does. Writes "Add a check for empty list." The author complies, the bug goes, and the next PR has the same gap, because the author learned one fix and no way of finding the next one.

What a senior does. Asks, when the author can find the answer by thinking or by running it: "What happens to the invoice total if lines is empty?" A question hands over the habit of checking, and it leaves room for the author to know something you don't. Google's guidance on comments makes this trade explicitly: pointing out problems and letting the developer decide "often helps the developer learn", and can give a better solution because the developer is closer to the code, while "sometimes direct instructions, suggestions, or even code are more helpful."

When not to ask. Be direct in two cases. A security hole is one: state it, block it, say why. The other is when you already know and the author can't find out from the code: a question then reads as a quiz. There's an honest middle form: "I think this throws on an empty list, because reduce has no initial value. Am I missing something?" And a question can be rude too. "Why would you do it this way?" is an order in costume. Ask about what the code does, not about the author's reasoning.

## Four orders, rewritten as questions

1. Order:    Add a check for an empty list.
   Question: What happens to the invoice total if `lines` is empty? Try it with an order where every line was refunded.

2. Order:    Wrap this in a transaction.
   Question: If the process dies between the `orders` insert and the `payments` insert, what does the customer see, and what does support see?

3. Order:    Add a LIMIT to this query.
   Question: How many rows does this return for our biggest customer, and what happens to the admin page when it does?

4. Order:    Don't retry on 4xx.
   Question: Which provider responses does this retry? What does the retry do after a 422 "already refunded"?

## When not to ask: say it directly

Security hole:
**issue (blocking):** This logs the full `Authorization` header, so API tokens end up in log storage. Please remove it before merge. Anything already logged from staging needs rotating.

You know something the code can't show:
**note:** The provider sends webhook amounts in piastres, not EGP. It isn't in the code or the types, so I'm telling you rather than asking: `12500` is 125.00 EGP.

  • Security hole، issue (blocking): "ده بيعمل log للـ Authorization header كله، يعني الـ API tokens هتتخزن في الـ logs. شيله قبل الـ merge، وأي token اتسجّل من staging لازم يتغيّر."
  • حاجة الكود مش هيوريها، note: "الـ provider بيبعت المبالغ في الـ webhooks بالقروش مش بالجنيه. ده مش موجود في الكود ولا الـ types، فبقولك بدل ما أسألك: 12500 يعني 125.00 جنيه."
فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ في الـ review الجاي، قبل ما تبعت أي comment بصيغة أمر، اسأل نفسك: صاحب الـ PR يقدر يوصل للإجابة لوحده في دقيقتين؟ لو آه، حوّله سؤال على سلوك الكود: edge case، crash في النص، حجم الـ data. لو الموضوع API key في log أو SQL injection، سيبك من الأسئلة وقولها صريح. الغلطة الشائعة إن السؤال يتحوّل لفخ وإنت عارف الإجابة ومخبيها.
Trade-off: questions cost a round trip On a PR that has to ship today, a question can cost a day. When timing matters, send the question and your best guess together ("I think X. Right?") or go direct. Save the pure question for when the learning is worth the wait.

Part 3 — Teaching instead of fixing 🟡 Intermediateالجزء 3 — تعلّم بدل ما تصلّح بنفسك

English

The situation. Salma's PR adds coupon redemption. It reads used_count, checks it against max_uses, then increments. Two checkouts at the same moment can both pass the check. You could fix it in five minutes.

What a mid-level engineer does. Pushes a commit to her branch and writes "fixed the race, good to merge." The bug is gone, and so is the lesson. Her PR now contains code she didn't write and can't explain, and read-then-write will show up again somewhere else in checkout.

What a senior does. Writes a comment that walks through the failure with real values, leaves the fix open, asks her to prove it with a failing test, then pairs for 15 minutes while she types. She gets to the answer herself: one conditional UPDATE ... WHERE used_count < max_uses, then check the row count. Databases lesson 16 compares the options.

Why it works. Google's guide says "it is the developer's responsibility to fix a CL, not the reviewer's", and names a second goal of review: "improving the skills of developers so that they require less and less review over time." The exception is production on fire: fix it now and explain afterwards. And ask before pushing to anyone's branch. For a one-line slip, GitHub's suggested change is fine.

**issue (blocking): Two customers can both get the last use of this coupon.**

Here's `redeemCoupon()` with two checkouts at the same moment, coupon `SAVE200`, `max_uses = 100`, `used_count = 99`:

    A: SELECT used_count  -> 99, passes the check
    B: SELECT used_count  -> 99, passes the check
    A: UPDATE used_count  -> 100
    B: UPDATE used_count  -> 101

That's 101 redemptions at 200 EGP each, and the check never failed. It needs two requests within a few milliseconds of each other, so a manual test won't show it. A sale will.

I could push a fix, and I'm choosing not to: this read-then-write shape is all over checkout, and it's worth knowing cold.

Two things, in this order:
1. Can you write a test that fires two redemptions in parallel and shows 101? If it passes, run it 20 times: races depend on timing.
2. Then let's take 15 minutes together and you drive. Free at 3? The options are compared in Databases lesson 16, part 1.

The situation. On day 9 of the work, Karim opens a 1,200-line PR moving order status changes to events. It touches POST /orders, the payment-webhook handler and both queue consumers.

What a mid-level engineer does. One of two things. Reviews it line by line, leaves thirty comments on details, and the structural problem hides behind them. Or finds it too big to read, and approves it. Either way nobody has looked at the design.

What a senior does. Doesn't start at line 1: "Let's talk for 15 minutes before I review this line by line." The question is a design one: the status write and the event publish are two separate steps, so a crash between them loses the event. Then they agree how to split the work. On the next piece of work, they ask for a one-paragraph design note or a draft PR on day 1.

Why it works. A design problem is cheapest to fix when the least has been built on top of it. Google's guidance is that "100 lines is usually a reasonable size for a CL, and 1000 lines is usually too large, but it's up to the judgment of your reviewer", and that files count too: 200 lines across 50 files would usually be too large. Those are Google's numbers for Google's codebase. Its speed guide says to ask for a split when a change is too big to review soon, and if it can't be split, to at least comment on the overall design.

Two ways to split. Refactor first, then change behaviour, so the first PR can be checked with the existing tests. Then split by layer behind a flag: table and writes first with nothing reading them, then one consumer at a time.

English

The situation. In the retro, two people say the same things: PRs wait for days, and nobody can tell which comments must be fixed. This is README artifact 5.

What a mid-level engineer does. Agrees in the retro, and nothing changes. Or goes away and writes a twelve-page handbook nobody asked for.

What a senior does. Writes one page that answers those two complaints and little else, proposes it as a PR so people can comment line by line, runs it as a 4-week trial, and measures time to first review before and after. At the end the team keeps what paid off and deletes the rest.

Why it works. Agreed rules turn "that's your taste" into "that's what we decided". A trial makes saying yes cheap, because nobody is committing forever. And a number at the end means the discussion is about what happened.

Automate what you can. A PR template fills the description for every PR. A CODEOWNERS file requests the right reviewers when a PR opens, and turning on "Require review from Code Owners" in branch protection makes it binding. Linters remove the style half of the page. The size numbers below are this invented team's choice: Google's are in Part 4, and yours should come from your own last 20 PRs. Lesson 04 covers the one-working-day response in depth.

# Code review at Orders

Status: 4-week trial, reviewed at the retro on 30 Nov. Owner: @you.
Why this exists: at the last retro we said PRs wait too long and we can't tell which comments matter.

## What review is for
In this order: design, correctness and edge cases, security, performance, tests.
Formatting is the linter's job. If a style point matters, add a rule.

## Labels
Conventional Comments: `issue`, `question`, `suggestion`, `nitpick`, `praise`, `note`, with `(blocking)` or `(non-blocking)`.
No label: treat it as non-blocking, and ask if unsure.

## What blocks a merge
- A bug, or a missing failure path: runs twice, empty input, crash half-way
- Money or personal data changed with no test on the failure path
- Security: missing authorization, injection, secrets or personal data in logs
- A change that's expensive to reverse with no agreement: schema, public API, event shape
- A migration with no rollback note

## What never blocks
Naming taste, file layout, "I'd have written it differently". Use `nitpick (non-blocking)` or let it go.

## Timing
First response within one working day. "I can't get to this before Thursday, ask Karim" counts.
A first response isn't an approval.
Review in your own slots. Nobody is expected to break off focused work.

## Size
Target: under 300 changed lines, not counting tests, generated files and lockfiles.
Over 600: agree a split first, or say in the description why it can't be split.
Work longer than 2 days: a paragraph or a draft PR on day 1.

## Who approves
- `src/payments/**`, `migrations/**`: @you or @hany
- `infra/**`, queue settings: @nour
- Everything else: any engineer on the team, including Salma

## When we disagree
Two rounds in the thread, then a 5-minute call. The outcome goes in the thread.
Still stuck: the code owner of the area decides. If they're part of the disagreement, Hany does.

## AI-generated code
Same bar, plus: the author can explain every line, and the PR says what the agent was asked to do.

## What's automated
- Prettier and ESLint in CI: style never reaches a human.
- `.github/pull_request_template.md`: what, why, how to test, what's out of scope.
- `.github/CODEOWNERS`: requests the reviewers listed above.

## How we'll judge the trial
Median hours from PR opened to first review. Last 20 PRs before the trial: 31 hours.
Poll at the retro: "Can you tell which comments must be fixed?"
We keep what helped and delete what didn't.

الأسبوع ده، هات 3 review comments سبتهم على PRs حقيقية وكانوا أوامر أو من غير سبب. اكتب كل واحد تاني في notes عندك، بإيدك مش بالـ AI، كسؤال أو ومعاه السبب. لو مفيش reviews قديمة عندك، اعملها على 3 comments من PR عام في open source، في الـ notes بس.
Hint · تلميح For each one, ask: could the author have found this alone in two minutes? Yes means a question about behaviour. No means say it directly, with the reason.
اسأل نفسك على كل واحد: صاحب الـ PR كان يقدر يوصل لده لوحده في دقيقتين؟ لو آه يبقى سؤال عن السلوك. لو لأ يبقى تقوله مباشرة ومعاه السبب.
Solution · الحل A worked example from the Orders team. Before: "Use a transaction here." After: "If the process dies between these two inserts, what does support see for this order?" Before: "This is slow." After: "This loads every line for each order, so a 50-row admin page runs 51 queries. It's our busiest page, so I'd batch it."
مثال من فريق Orders. قبل: "استخدم transaction هنا". بعد: "لو الـ process وقع بين الـ insert الاتنين دول، الـ support هيشوف إيه للـ order ده؟". قبل: "ده بطيء". بعد: "ده بيحمّل كل البنود لكل order، فصفحة admin فيها 50 row بتعمل 51 query. ودي أكتر صفحة عليها ضغط، فأنا كنت هعمل batch."
2. Review a plan on day 1 🟡 medium Ask one person this week to share a draft PR or a one-paragraph plan on the first day of their next task, and review that: direction only, no line comments. Write the words yourself, without AI. If nobody can do it this week, write a one-paragraph plan for your side project in project/ and ask a friend to read it the same way.
اطلب من شخص واحد الأسبوع ده يشاركك draft PR أو خطة في فقرة واحدة في أول يوم من الـ task الجاية بتاعته، وراجعها على الاتجاه بس، من غير line comments. اكتب الكلام بنفسك مش بالـ AI. لو مفيش حد ينفع الأسبوع ده، اكتب فقرة خطة لمشروعك الجانبي في project/ واطلب من صاحبك يقراها بنفس الطريقة.
Solution · الحل A worked example. You to Karim: "Could you send me a paragraph on the coupon-stacking work before you start? I'll only look at the direction." His paragraph says coupons will apply in the order they were entered. Your reply: "Direction looks right. One question: what does a customer pay if two coupons each take 50%? If the answer depends on order, that's a product decision, so let's check it with Mona before you build." You've changed the work on day 1 for the price of three lines.
مثال محلول. إنت لكريم: "ممكن تبعتلي فقرة عن الـ coupon stacking قبل ما تبدأ؟ هبص على الاتجاه بس." الفقرة بتقول الـ coupons هتتطبق بترتيب إدخالها. ردك: "الاتجاه شكله صح. سؤال واحد: العميل هيدفع كام لو اتنين coupon كل واحد بياخد 50%؟ لو الإجابة بتعتمد على الترتيب، يبقى ده قرار product، فنتأكد مع منى قبل ما تبني." كده غيّرت الشغل في يوم 1 بـ 3 سطور.
3. Write your team's guidelines (README artifact 5) 🔴 at work Draft a one-page guideline by hand, starting from a complaint your team has actually made, and propose it as a PR for a 4-week trial. Record the median time to first review for your last 20 PRs first. If you can't propose it at work, write it as CONTRIBUTING.md for your side project.
اكتب بإيدك صفحة guidelines واحدة، وابدأ من شكوى قالها فريقك فعلًا، واقترحها كـ PR لـ trial مدته 4 أسابيع. سجّل الأول الـ median للوقت لأول review في آخر 20 PR. لو مش هينفع تقترحها في الشغل، اكتبها CONTRIBUTING.md لمشروعك الجانبي.
Hint · تلميح Before writing anything, ask two teammates what annoys them most about reviews here. Their words go at the top of the document. Leave out any section nobody complained about.
قبل ما تكتب أي حاجة، اسأل اتنين زمايلك إيه أكتر حاجة بتضايقهم في الـ reviews عندكم. كلامهم يتحط في أول الملف. وأي جزء محدش اشتكى منه سيبه.
Solution · الحل A worked example: the Orders team's page in Part 5. At the end of the invented trial: median time to first review went from 31 hours to 9, so the timing rule stays. Nobody used the 600-line rule, so it's deleted. The poll showed people still weren't sure about question, so "questions are blocking only if marked" is added.
مثال محلول: صفحة فريق Orders في الجزء 5. في آخر الـ trial المتألّف: الـ median للوقت لأول review نزل من 31 ساعة لـ 9، فقاعدة التوقيت هتفضل. ومحدش استخدم قاعدة الـ 600 سطر، فهتتمسح. والتصويت بيّن إن الناس لسه مش متأكدة من question، فهنضيف "الأسئلة مش blocking إلا لو مكتوب كده".

Cheat sheet · الخلاصة

  • Read in order of cost to fix later: design, correctness and edge cases, security, performance, tests. Names only when they mislead. Formatting belongs to the linter.
  • "What happens if this runs twice?" beats nine naming comments.
  • If you stop commenting on style, add the rule.
  • Ask when the author can find the answer; be direct on security holes or things the code can't show. "I think X, am I missing something?" is the honest middle.
  • Point at the failure with real values and let the author fix it. Production on fire is the exception: fix first, explain after.
  • Huge PR: talk about the design before line 1, then split: refactor first, then layers behind a flag. Ask for a paragraph or a draft on day 1 next time.
  • Google's numbers, as Google's guidance: 100 lines usually reasonable, 1000 usually too large, reviewer's judgement, file spread counts.
  • Guidelines: one page, starts from a real complaint, proposed as a PR, 4-week trial, measure time to first review, delete what didn't help.
  • AI code: the author explains every line, the reviewer sees the spec. Green tests from the same prompt prove little.
  • Thread stuck after two rounds: 5-minute call, outcome in the thread with decision, reason and decider. No agreement: the named decider decides.
بالعامية
  • اقرا بترتيب تكلفة التصليح بعدين: الـ design، الـ correctness والـ edge cases، الـ security، الـ performance، الـ tests. الأسماء بس لما تضلّل. والـ formatting شغل الـ linter.
  • "لو اشتغلت مرتين هيحصل إيه؟" أحسن من 9 comments على الأسماء.
  • لو بطّلت تعلّق على الـ style، ضيف الـ rule.
  • اسأل لما صاحب الـ PR يقدر يوصل للإجابة، وخليك مباشر في الـ security أو الحاجات اللي الكود مش هيوريها. و"أنا شايف كذا، فايتني حاجة؟" هي الحل الوسط الصريح.
  • شاور على الـ failure بأرقام حقيقية وسيب صاحب الـ PR يصلّح. الاستثناء الـ production لو والعة: صلّح الأول واشرح بعدين.
  • PR ضخم: اتكلم عن الـ design قبل أول سطر، وبعدين قسّم: refactor الأول، وبعدين layers ورا flag. والمرة الجاية اطلب فقرة أو draft من يوم 1.
  • أرقام Google، كإرشادات Google: 100 سطر غالبًا معقول، 1000 غالبًا كتير، والقرار تقدير الـ reviewer، وعدد الملفات بيفرق.
  • الـ guidelines: صفحة واحدة، بتبدأ من شكوى حقيقية، بتتقترح كـ PR، trial لـ 4 أسابيع، تقيس الوقت لأول review، وتمسح اللي منفعش.
  • كود الـ AI: صاحب الـ PR يشرح كل سطر، والـ reviewer يشوف الـ spec. والـ tests الخضرا من نفس الـ prompt مش بتثبت كتير.
  • thread واقف بعد جولتين: call 5 دقايق، والنتيجة تتكتب في الـ thread بالقرار والسبب ومين قرر. ومفيش اتفاق؟ الشخص المحدد بالاسم يقرر.
