Why this matters · ليه ده مهم

English

Behavioral rounds are decided by evidence of what you did, and people lose them because they answer from memory on the day: a vague "we fixed it" with no number and no decision. A prepared bank of real stories, each one rehearsed out loud to a length you've measured, turns that round from improvisation into recall. It also pays off outside interviews, since the same stories feed your CV (lesson 02), your promotion case and your harder behavioral answers (lesson 11).

Part 1 — STAR anatomy 🟢 Basicالجزء 1 — تشريح الـ STAR

⏱ Timed prompt: 2 minutes Out loud, no notes: "Tell me about a production problem you owned." Use a real one from your own work and record it on your phone. Stop at 2 minutes. Then read on.
بصوت عالي ومن غير ورقة: "احكيلي عن مشكلة production إنت كنت مسؤول عنها". استخدم مشكلة حقيقية من شغلك إنت وسجّلها على الموبايل. وقّف عند دقيقتين. وبعدها كمّل قراية.
English

Model solution. STAR is Situation, Task, Action, Result. Situation is the context and what was at stake, in a sentence or two. Task is what you were responsible for, and not what the team was. Action is what you did, in order, including what you considered and rejected: it should be about 60% of the words. Result is what changed, with numbers if numbers exist.

Write the Action in the first person. "I" tells the interviewer what you can do again next month, and "we" tells them your team shipped something. The README's 60% is this curriculum's rule of thumb, not a published figure. The Tech Interview Handbook gives no percentage, though it does say to "pay special attention to the Actions". It adds a fifth step, Reflection, and calls that "an additional R": what you learned and do differently now, which is what makes a story sound Senior.

The same handbook advises collecting "3-5 projects that have high impact, high complexity, and high involvement by you". The README's 8–10 stories go further, because 3 to 5 projects won't cover all ten themes in the next part. A project can provide more than one story, so the two pieces of advice fit together.

star-lint.ts gives you something to observe. It splits a story on S:, T:, A:, R: and REF: markers, then prints the word share of each section, "I" against "we" in the Action, and digits in the Result. It's a rough heuristic that counts words and doesn't judge the story: a story can pass and still be weak, or fail and still be good. ❌ Weak: story 1, the way it comes out unprepared

S: We had a problem with refunds at my company because of some webhooks.

T: We needed to fix it quickly because customers were complaining a lot.

A: We looked into the logs and we saw the webhook was retrying. The team worked on it over the weekend and we added some checks and we deployed the fix.

R: It was fixed and everyone was happy.

✅ Strong: same story (fictional, Mariam)

S: At OrderLane, a B2B order-management SaaS, our payment provider retries a webhook when our endpoint times out. Over one weekend those retries caused 37 duplicate refunds.

T: The webhook had no clear owner, so I took it: stop the duplicates, fix the cause, and make sure it could not come back.

A: First I switched refunds to manual approval so the duplicates stopped while I investigated. Then I grouped refunds by payment and provider event, which gave me the list of 37 and showed that every duplicate shared one event id. That became my hypothesis: our handler was not idempotent. I replayed one event in staging and reproduced a second refund, which confirmed it. I wrote the fix in two layers: an idempotency key taken from the provider's event id, and a unique constraint on tenant and key, because an application check alone still races when two retries land together. I added a test that fires the same event twice at the same moment. Then I went through the 37 refunds with finance to reconcile them, and I wrote up the timeline so the other webhook handlers would get the same review.

R: All 37 duplicates were reconciled, and in the next 90 days we had 0 duplicate refunds.

REF: I learned to treat a retry as the normal path. I now ask "what happens if this runs twice?" in every webhook review.

import { readFileSync } from "node:fs";

export type SectionKey = "S" | "T" | "A" | "R" | "REF";
const KEYS: readonly SectionKey[] = ["S", "T", "A", "R", "REF"];

// كل قسم بيبدأ بسطر زي "A: ..." لحد القسم اللي بعده
export function parseStar(text: string): Partial<Record<SectionKey, string>> {
  const found: Partial<Record<SectionKey, string>> = {};
  let current: SectionKey | null = null;
  for (const line of text.split(/\r?\n/)) {
    const match = /^(S|T|A|R|REF):\s*(.*)$/.exec(line.trim());
    if (match) {
      current = match[1] as SectionKey;
      found[current] = match[2] ?? "";
    } else if (current && line.trim() !== "") {
      found[current] += " " + line.trim();
    }
  }
  return found;
}

const words = (text = ""): string[] => text.split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean);
const countMatches = (text: string, re: RegExp): number => (text.match(re) ?? []).length;

export type Report = {
  total: number;
  share: Record<SectionKey, number>;
  iCount: number;
  weCount: number;
  resultDigits: number;
  warnings: string[];
};

export function lint(text: string): Report {
  const sections = parseStar(text);
  const counts = Object.fromEntries(KEYS.map((k) => [k, words(sections[k]).length])) as Record<SectionKey, number>;
  const total = KEYS.reduce((sum, k) => sum + counts[k], 0);
  const share = Object.fromEntries(
    KEYS.map((k) => [k, total === 0 ? 0 : Math.round((counts[k] / total) * 100)]),
  ) as Record<SectionKey, number>;

  const action = sections.A ?? "";
  const iCount = countMatches(action, /\b(I|I'm|I've|my|me)\b/g);
  const weCount = countMatches(action, /\b(we|we're|we've|our|us|the team)\b/gi);
  const resultDigits = countMatches(sections.R ?? "", /\d+/g);

  const warnings: string[] = [];
  for (const k of ["S", "T", "A", "R"] as const) {
    if (counts[k] === 0) warnings.push(`missing section ${k}`);
  }
  if (share.A < 45) warnings.push(`Action is only ${share.A}% of the words`);
  if (share.A > 70) warnings.push(`Action is ${share.A}% of the words: is the context missing?`);
  if (weCount > iCount) warnings.push(`Action says "we" ${weCount} times against "I" ${iCount}`);
  if (resultDigits === 0) warnings.push("no digit in Result: measure it, estimate it and say how, or say it was not measured");
  if (counts.REF === 0) warnings.push("no reflection (REF:) line");
  return { total, share, iCount, weCount, resultDigits, warnings };
}

export function format(report: Report): string {
  const rows = KEYS.map((k) => `  ${k.padEnd(4)}${String(report.share[k]).padStart(4)}%`);
  return [
    `words: ${report.total}`,
    ...rows,
    `I in Action: ${report.iCount}   we in Action: ${report.weCount}   digits in Result: ${report.resultDigits}`,
    report.warnings.length === 0 ? "warnings: none" : `warnings (${report.warnings.length}):`,
    ...report.warnings.map((w) => `  - ${w}`),
  ].join("\n");
}

if (process.argv[1]?.endsWith("star-lint.ts")) {
  const files = process.argv.slice(2);
  if (files.length === 0) {
    console.error("usage: node star-lint.ts <story.txt> [more.txt]");
    process.exit(2);
  }
  for (const file of files) {
    console.log(`=== ${file}`);
    console.log(format(lint(readFileSync(file, "utf8"))));
  }
}

بالعامية

الحل النموذجي. الـ STAR يعني Situation و Task و Action و Result. الـ Situation السياق وإيه اللي كان على المحك، في جملة أو اتنين. الـ Task إنت كنت مسؤول عن إيه، مش الفريق. الـ Action اللي عملته بالترتيب، وإيه اللي فكرت فيه ورفضته: المفروض يبقى حوالي 60% من الكلام. والـ Result إيه اللي اتغير، بالأرقام لو الأرقام موجودة.

اكتب الـ Action بصيغة "أنا". "أنا" بتقول للـ interviewer إنت هتقدر تعمل إيه تاني الشهر الجاي، و"إحنا" بتقول إن فريقك عمل حاجة. الـ 60% اللي في الـ README هي rule of thumb في المنهج ده، مش رقم منشور. الـ Tech Interview Handbook مبيديش نسبة، بس بيقول "pay special attention to the Actions" يعني خلي بالك من الـ Actions بالذات. وبيضيف خطوة خامسة، الـ Reflection، وبيسميها "an additional R": إنت اتعلمت إيه وبتعمل إيه بشكل مختلف دلوقتي، ودي اللي بتخلي القصة تبان سنيور.

نفس الـ handbook بينصح تجمع "3-5 projects" فيها impact عالي وتعقيد عالي ودور كبير ليك. الـ 8–10 قصص اللي في الـ README أكتر من كده، لأن 3 لـ 5 مشاريع مش هتغطي الـ 10 themes اللي في الجزء الجاي. والمشروع الواحد ممكن يطلّع أكتر من قصة، فالنصيحتين ماشيين مع بعض.

star-lint.ts بيديك حاجة تقدر تبص عليها. بيقسم القصة على العلامات S: وT: وA: وR: وREF:، وبيطبع نسبة كلام كل قسم، و"أنا" قصاد "إحنا" في الـ Action، والأرقام في الـ Result. ده heuristic تقريبي بيعد كلمات ومش بيحكم على القصة: قصة ممكن تعدّي وتبقى ضعيفة، أو تفشل وتبقى كويسة.

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ لما تخلص incident أو feature تقيل، اكتب الـ STAR بتاعها في 5 سطور في نفس اليوم في stories.md، والأرقام لسه طازة في الـ dashboard أو الـ postmortem. لو استنيت لحد ما تدوّر على شغل هتلاقي نفسك بتقول "حوالي" وبتنسى إيه اللي رفضته. وحط الـ S و T و A و R على سطور منفصلة عشان تقدر تشغّل عليهم الـ linter.

Part 2 — Ten themes 🟢 Basicالجزء 2 — الـ 10 مواضيع

⏱ Timed prompt: 15 minutes On paper, write the ten themes below and one line per theme: the real story you'd tell for it. Leave a blank where you have nothing. The blanks are the useful output of this exercise. Don't fill them with something that didn't happen.
على ورقة، اكتب الـ 10 مواضيع اللي تحت، وسطر لكل موضوع: القصة الحقيقية اللي هتحكيها. سيب فراغ لو مفيش عندك حاجة. الفراغات دي هي الناتج المفيد من التمرين. ومتملاهاش بحاجة محصلتش.
English

Model solution. The ten themes are ownership; conflict with a colleague, manager or product; failure and what you learned; influence without authority; mentoring; incident or pressure; a hard trade-off; a technical decision you corrected; a scope cut; and an impossible deadline. Mariam's bank has one story per theme, in a line each below. She then writes the full STAR for three of them: ownership (1), conflict (4) and failure (3).

If you have no real story for a theme, say so in your notes and go earn one at work. Section 15 teaches how: incidents, cross-team influence, mentoring. Don't invent it. An interviewer's second follow-up question dismantles an invented story, and it's the opposite of what this lesson is for.

Full STAR: story 3, failure (fictional, Mariam)

S: At OrderLane I shipped a migration during a normal weekday deploy. It changed a column type on the orders table, which rewrites the whole table under an exclusive lock.

import { readFileSync } from "node:fs";

export type Question = { id: string; text: string };
export type Story = { id: string; title: string; answers: string[] };
export type Bank = { questions: Question[]; stories: Story[]; maxQuestionsPerStory: number };

export type Matrix = {
  coverage: Map<string, string[]>;
  thin: Question[];
  overloaded: Story[];
  unknown: string[];
};

export function buildMatrix(bank: Bank): Matrix {
  const coverage = new Map<string, string[]>(bank.questions.map((q) => [q.id, []]));
  const unknown: string[] = [];
  for (const story of bank.stories) {
    for (const questionId of story.answers) {
      const list = coverage.get(questionId);
      if (list === undefined) unknown.push(`${story.id} -> ${questionId}`);
      else list.push(story.id);
    }
  }
  return {
    coverage,
    unknown,
    thin: bank.questions.filter((q) => (coverage.get(q.id) ?? []).length < 2),
    overloaded: bank.stories.filter((s) => s.answers.length > bank.maxQuestionsPerStory),
  };
}

export function render(bank: Bank, m: Matrix): string {
  const header = "question".padEnd(46) + bank.stories.map((s) => s.id.padStart(4)).join("") + "  n";
  const rows = bank.questions.map((q) => {
    const cells = bank.stories.map((s) => (s.answers.includes(q.id) ? "   x" : "   .")).join("");
    return `${q.id} ${q.text}`.padEnd(46) + cells + "  " + (m.coverage.get(q.id) ?? []).length;
  });
  const lines = [header, ...rows, ""];
  lines.push(`questions with fewer than 2 stories (${m.thin.length}):`);
  for (const q of m.thin) lines.push(`  ${q.id} ${q.text}: ${(m.coverage.get(q.id) ?? []).join(", ") || "none"}`);
  lines.push(`stories carrying more than ${bank.maxQuestionsPerStory} questions (${m.overloaded.length}):`);
  for (const s of m.overloaded) lines.push(`  ${s.id} ${s.title}: ${s.answers.length}`);
  if (m.unknown.length > 0) lines.push(`unknown question ids: ${m.unknown.join(", ")}`);
  return lines.join("\n");
}

Model solution. One page per story is the README artifact stories.md. Writing it forces the numbers, the order, and the alternatives you rejected, and those are exactly the things you can't reconstruct under pressure.

Measure your own pace. People speak at very different rates, and there's no universal words-per-minute figure worth using for this. wpm.ts takes the word count of what you said and your stopwatch time, prints your pace, and turns it into a word budget for a two-minute answer and for a 90-second intro. Run it on all three takes, because it uses the median so that one rough take doesn't distort it. In the fictional sample below, Mariam timed 268 words in 2:06, 251 in 1:58 and 274 in 2:11, so her budget is 256 words for two minutes. Story 1 is 229 words, which fits; story 3 is 254, which fits with almost nothing to spare.

Practice protocol. Put cue words on a card, not memorized prose. Record three takes. After each take, note what you dropped, and put back only what mattered. A memorized answer sounds recited and collapses the moment you're interrupted, while cues survive a follow-up question halfway through. export type Take = { words: number; seconds: number };

export function parseClock(text: string): number {
  const match = /^(\d+):([0-5]\d)$/.exec(text);
  if (match === null) throw new RangeError(`expected m:ss, got "${text}"`);
  return Number(match[1]) * 60 + Number(match[2]);
}

export function wordsPerMinute({ words, seconds }: Take): number {
  if (!Number.isInteger(words) || words <= 0) throw new RangeError(`bad word count: ${words}`);
  if (seconds <= 0) throw new RangeError(`bad duration: ${seconds}`);
  return (words / seconds) * 60;
}

// الـ median عشان take واحدة بطيئة متبوّظش الرقم
export function medianPace(takes: readonly Take[]): number {
  if (takes.length === 0) throw new RangeError("need at least one take");
  const paces = takes.map(wordsPerMinute).sort((a, b) => a - b);
  const mid = Math.floor(paces.length / 2);
  return paces.length % 2 === 1 ? paces[mid]! : (paces[mid - 1]! + paces[mid]!) / 2;
}

export const budget = (pace: number, seconds: number): number => Math.floor((pace * seconds) / 60);

export function starBudget(pace: number, seconds: number) {
  const total = budget(pace, seconds);
  const action = Math.round(total * 0.6);
  const context = Math.round(total * 0.2);
  return { total, situationAndTask: context, action, resultAndReflection: total - action - context };
}

Four things get scored: scope (how big the thing you changed was), ownership (what you did and what you carried), self-awareness (what you got wrong and changed), and evidence (numbers, or a clear statement that there are none). The red flags are blaming others, "we did" with no "I", no result, no reflection, naming a customer, and sharing confidential numbers from your employer. red-flags.ts scans a story for those. It's a rough heuristic, it only flags, and a person hearing you is the real judge.

Confidentiality is a separate check, and it's yours. A customer name or a private revenue figure in a story tells the interviewer you'd do the same with their data. Say "a large retail customer". Replace a private figure with what you're allowed to share: a percentage, a rough magnitude, or "I can't share the figure".

AI. AI may help you phrase a story, but it can't supply the experience. Check each company's rule. As of 2026-11-09, Datadog's public candidate guidelines say that using AI for a resume or cover letter is acceptable, that "we expect the application materials you submit to represent your qualifications and skills accurately", and "don't include any ideas or answers that you wouldn't be comfortable explaining". That's a good standard everywhere: if you can't explain a sentence in your own words, take it out.

import { readFileSync } from "node:fs";
import { lint } from "./star-lint.ts";

export type DenyList = { customers: string[]; confidential: string[] };

const BLAME = /\b(their fault|not my (fault|job)|(they|he|she|QA|ops|the PM) (never|didn't|did not|missed|failed))\b/gi;
const MONEY = /[$€£]\s?\d[\d,.]*\s?[kKmM]?/g;

const escapeRe = (s: string) => s.replace(/[.*+?^${}()|[\]\\]/g, "\\$&");

// بيطلّع علامات خطر بس، مش حكم على القصة: اللي بيسمعك هو اللي بيقيّم
export function scan(text: string, deny: DenyList): string[] {
  const flags: string[] = [];
  for (const hit of text.match(BLAME) ?? []) flags.push(`blame: "${hit}"`);

  const report = lint(text);
  if (report.weCount > report.iCount) flags.push(`"we" (${report.weCount}) beats "I" (${report.iCount}) in Action`);
  if (report.resultDigits === 0) flags.push("no number in Result");
  if (report.share.REF === 0) flags.push("no reflection");

  for (const name of deny.customers) {
    if (new RegExp(escapeRe(name), "i").test(text)) flags.push(`names a customer: "${name}"`);
  }
  for (const term of deny.confidential) {
    if (new RegExp(`\\b${escapeRe(term)}\\b`, "i").test(text)) flags.push(`confidential term: "${term}"`);
  }
  for (const hit of text.match(MONEY) ?? []) flags.push(`money figure: "${hit.trim()}"`);
  return flags;
}

الحل النموذجي. بيتقيّم 4 حاجات: الـ scope (حجم الحاجة اللي غيّرتها)، الـ ownership (اللي عملته واللي شلته)، الـ self-awareness (اللي غلطت فيه وغيّرته)، والـ evidence (أرقام، أو توضيح صريح إنها مش موجودة). الـ red flags هي: تلوم الناس، "إحنا عملنا" من غير "أنا"، مفيش نتيجة، مفيش reflection، تسمّي عميل، وتشارك أرقام سرية من شغلك. red-flags.ts بيدوّر على ده في القصة. ده heuristic تقريبي بيطلّع علامات بس، واللي بيسمعك هو الحكم الحقيقي.

السرية شغلك إنت، وفحص منفصل. اسم عميل أو رقم إيرادات سري في قصة بيقول للـ interviewer إنك هتعمل نفس الحكاية مع بياناتهم. قول "عميل retail كبير". وبدّل الرقم السري باللي مسموح تقوله: نسبة، أو حجم تقريبي، أو "مش هقدر أشارك الرقم".

الـ AI. الـ AI ممكن يساعدك تصيغ قصة، بس ميقدرش يديك الخبرة. راجع قواعد كل شركة. لحد 2026-11-09، إرشادات Datadog المعلنة للمتقدمين بتقول إن استخدام الـ AI في الـ resume أو الـ cover letter مقبول، وإنهم متوقعين إن المواد اللي بتقدمها تمثل مؤهلاتك ومهاراتك بدقة، و"don't include any ideas or answers that you wouldn't be comfortable explaining" يعني متحطش أفكار أو إجابات مش مرتاح تشرحها. ودي قاعدة كويسة في كل مكان: لو مش قادر تشرح جملة بكلامك، شيلها.

فين هستخدمه في شغلي؟ قبل أي interview، شغّل red-flags.ts بملف deny.json فيه أسماء عملاء شغلك والأرقام اللي متتقالش، والملف ده يفضل على جهازك بس، وعمره ما يتحط في repo عام. وبعد كل mock اسأل اللي بيسمعك: "مين في القصة دي كان بيعمل إيه؟". لو ماعرفش يقول إنت عملت إيه، الـ "إحنا" كسبت.

الغلطة إنك تستخدم رقم سري عشان تبان قوي. "أكبر عميل عندنا، بيدفع كذا في السنة، مشي" بيبيع ثقة شغلك عشان قصة شكلها أقوى. بدّله باللي آمن يتقال، ولو القصة مش بتصمد من غيره، اختار قصة تانية.

Common mistakes · غلطات شائعة

❌ "We"

We looked into the logs and we saw the webhook was retrying. We added some checks and we deployed the fix.

✅ "I", with a decision

I grouped refunds by provider event and found every duplicate shared one event id. I added an idempotency key and a unique constraint, because an application check alone still races.

بالعامية

❌ "إحنا بصينا في الـ logs، وإحنا ضفنا checks": الجملة مفيهاش حد، والـ interviewer مش عارف مين عمل إيه. ✅ "أنا جمعت الـ refunds حسب الـ event، ولقيت كل duplicate ليه نفس الـ event id، وضفت idempotency key و unique constraint لأن الفحص في الكود لوحده لسه فيه race": خطوات، وقرار، وسبب. (مثال مريم المتخيلة، متستخدمش تفاصيلها.)

❌ A number with nothing behind it

I improved performance by about 80%.

(Where did 80% come from? Of what? The interviewer asks, and the answer falls apart.)

✅ Three honest options

Measured: "Checkout p99 went from 900 ms to 140 ms, from our traces."

Estimated: "Roughly a third fewer support tickets, from counting one month of them by hand."

Not measured: "We didn't measure it. What I can say is that it stopped showing up in the on-call handover."

بالعامية

❌ "حسنت الأداء حوالي 80%": الرقم جاي منين؟ 80% من إيه؟ أول سؤال متابعة بيوقعها. ✅ 3 حلول أمينة: مقاس: "الـ checkout p99 نزل من 900 ms لـ 140 ms من الـ traces" (رقم مريم المتخيل). تقدير وبطريقة: "حوالي تلت tickets الـ support أقل، عديتهم بإيدي لمدة شهر" (مثال). ماتقاسش: "ماقسناهوش، بس اللي أقدر أقوله إنه بطل يظهر في الـ on-call handover". عمرك ما تخترع رقم عشان الجملة تبقى أقوى.

❌ Blame in the failure story

The migration locked the table because nobody told me it rewrites the whole table, and QA didn't test on real data.

✅ Owning it

I shipped a migration that rewrote the table under a lock. I'd only tested it on a staging table far smaller than production, and I hadn't checked what lock it takes. Here's what I changed so it can't repeat.

بالعامية

❌ "محدش قالي إنها بتعيد كتابة الجدول، والـ QA مجربش على بيانات حقيقية": بتلوم ناس مش موجودين، والـ interviewer بيستنتج إنك هتلومه هو كمان. ✅ "أنا نزّلت migration عملت rewrite للجدول تحت lock. جربتها على staging أصغر بكتير ومبصيتش على الـ lock. وده اللي غيرته عشان متتكررش": مسؤولية، وسبب حقيقي، وتغيير بعدها. (تفاصيل مريم المتخيلة، متستخدمهاش.)

Cheat sheet · الخلاصة

  • STAR: Situation (stakes, short) → Task (your job) → Action (about 60% of the words, in order, with what you rejected) → Result (a number, an estimate and how, or "not measured"). Add a Reflection.
  • Say "I" for what you did, and name what the team did separately.
  • Ten themes: ownership, conflict, failure, influence, mentoring, incident, trade-off, corrected decision, scope cut, impossible deadline.
  • No real story for a theme: write NO STORY YET and go earn it. Don't invent one.
  • One story answers several questions; aim for at least 2 stories per question and no story on too many.
  • One page per story in stories.md. Cue words on a card, three recorded takes, note what you dropped.
  • Measure your own pace with wpm.ts: there's no universal words-per-minute figure.
  • Tell me about yourself in 90 seconds: present → the past that matters here → why this company specifically.
  • They score scope, ownership, self-awareness and evidence.
  • Red flags: blame, "we did", no result, no reflection, customer names, confidential numbers.
  • AI can help you phrase a story and can't supply the experience. If you can't explain a sentence, cut it.
