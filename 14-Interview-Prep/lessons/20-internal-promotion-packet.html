Why this matters · ليه ده مهم

English

An internal promotion is an interview whose panel already has an opinion of you. It has a rubric (the leveling guide), a loop (your manager and whoever else decides), follow-up questions and a decision. The only difference is that you can prepare for months, and most people don't.

The craft of the brag document, the packet and the first conversation belongs to section 13: lesson 21, items 3.5 and 3.6. This lesson drills what section 13 can't: defending the case under questions, and deciding in advance when you would stop waiting. Everything here is advice unless a source is named, and how promotion works varies a lot by company.

الترقية الداخلية interview، بس الـ panel عنده رأي فيك من الأول. فيها rubric (الـ leveling guide)، وloop (الـ manager وأي حد تاني بيقرر)، وأسئلة متابعة، وقرار. الفرق الوحيد إنك تقدر تجهز شهور، ومعظم الناس مبيعملوش ده.

Part 1 — The leveling guide as a rubric 🔴 Advancedالجزء 1 — الـ leveling guide كـ rubric

⏱ Timed prompt · 15 minutes Take your company's leveling guide. If it doesn't have one, use a public framework as a stand-in. Pick five expectations for the level you want. For each one, write the question it is really asking, the story you would answer with, and what's missing.
تمرين بتوقيت · 15 دقيقة. خد الـ leveling guide بتاع شركتك. لو مفيش، استخدم framework عام مكانه. اختار 5 expectations للمستوى اللي عايزه. لكل واحدة اكتب السؤال اللي بتسأله فعلًا، والقصة اللي هتجاوب بيها، وإيه الناقص.
English

Model solution. Finding the real bar and getting expectations agreed in writing are in section 13 · 3.5. New here is the reading: treat each expectation the way an interviewer treats a scorecard line, as a question you must answer with a story from your story bank.

Dropbox publishes its career framework, which makes a useful public example. It isn't a standard: titles and levels don't map across companies, and Dropbox itself says it "is not a promotion checklist". Look at how one line changes between two of its levels: IC3 says "I own and deliver projects in service of quarterly goals on the team", and IC4 says "I own and deliver semi-annual/annual goals for my team". Same verb, different horizon. So the question a reviewer asks moves from "which project?" to "which part of the year was yours?".

gaps.ts holds each expectation with its question, evidence, matching story and status, and prints what you can't answer yet. It also refuses to believe a status you can't back up: "met" with no evidence is reported as a problem. It's a rough heuristic that doesn't judge the story, because it only counts what you typed. Mariam's sheet below is the example; the empty template after it is yours.

English

Model solution. Building the packet, expectation to evidence (RFC, incident, mentee, owned feature), with StaffEng's sections adapted from Staff-plus to Senior, is in section 13 · 3.5. Don't rebuild it here. New here are the five cold questions a reviewer asks, and what happens to your packet under them.

A reviewer doesn't have to be hostile for these to hurt. They are looking for the things a packet can hide: other people's work written up as yours, an outcome that would have happened anyway, and work that's good but not at the next level. The third is the hardest question, and the answer is about scope and judgment, not effort.

packet-lint.ts checks that every expectation has a linked item, a statement of your part, and an honest measurement status. It accepts three kinds of measurement on purpose: measured, estimated with a method, or not measured with a reason. There's no fourth option where you round up. It's a rough heuristic: it checks structure, and it can't judge whether the story holds.

The same evidence is what you'd use outside: each packet line becomes a CV bullet and a STAR story, so the work isn't lost if the promotion doesn't come.

import { readFileSync } from "node:fs";

// الرقم مش لازم يبقى موجود، بس لازم تقول حالته بصراحة
type Measurement =
  | { kind: "measured"; value: string; how: string }
  | { kind: "estimate"; value: string; how: string }
  | { kind: "not-measured"; why: string };

type Item = {
  expectation: string;
  link: string | null;
  myPart: string | null;
  measurement: Measurement | null;
};

const items = JSON.parse(readFileSync(process.argv[2], "utf8")) as Item[];
let warnings = 0;

function warn(message: string): void {
  warnings++;
  console.log(`  ! ${message}`);
}

console.log(`${process.argv[2]}: ${items.length} expectations`);
for (const item of items) {
  console.log(item.expectation);
  if (!item.link) warn("no linked item: a reviewer can't open anything");
  if (!item.myPart) {
    warn("doesn't say what YOUR part was");
  } else if (/\bwe\b/i.test(item.myPart) && !/\bI\b/.test(item.myPart)) {
    // "إحنا" لوحدها مبتقولش إنت عملت إيه
    warn('says "we" and never "I"');
  }
  const m = item.measurement;
  if (m === null) {
    warn("no measurement: measure it, estimate and say how, or say it wasn't measured");
  } else if (m.kind === "estimate" && m.how.trim() === "") {
    warn("estimate with no method");
  }
}
console.log(warnings === 0 ? "\nevery expectation has a link, your part and a measurement status" : `\n${warnings} warning(s)`);

English

Model solution. The README says do the Senior job for at least six months before the title. Take that as advice: the number isn't universal and companies differ. The reasoning behind it is sound, though. Dropbox's public promotion principles say promotions are "a recognition of demonstrated, sustained impact at the next level", and "we don't promote someone because we hope they can handle it". What gets assessed is a pattern over time.

So what does a six-month record look like? Evidence in most months, across several expectations, not just a burst before review. The brag document itself is in section 13 · 3.5. brag.ts reads that log and counts evidence per expectation per month, then warns about empty months, a span shorter than six months, and a record that is mostly the last two months. A rough heuristic: it counts entries and can't weigh them.

One caution: doing the job ahead of the title is a means, not a permanent state. If the evidence is there and the title still doesn't come, that's Part 6.

// فحص تقريبي (rough heuristic): بيبص على شكل الطلب والرد، مش على صحتهم
type Ask = { to: string; text: string };
type Received = { from: string; said: string; myReply: string; action: string | null; due: string | null };

function lintAsk(ask: Ask): string[] {
  const out: string[] = [];
  if (/\bany feedback\b/i.test(ask.text)) out.push('generic "any feedback?" gets a generic answer');
  if (!/\b(RFC|incident|PR|postmortem|review|design|migration)\b/.test(ask.text)) out.push("names no specific piece of work");
  if (!/\?/.test(ask.text)) out.push("asks no actual question");
  if (!/\b(by|before)\s+\d{1,2}\s+[A-Z][a-z]+/.test(ask.text)) out.push("no date to answer by");
  return out;
}

// الرد الدفاعي بيقفل الباب: اللي قال الملاحظة مش هيقول تاني
const DEFENSIVE = /\b(but|actually|however|not fair|you don't understand|that's because)\b/i;

function toGapLine(r: Received): string {
  const flags: string[] = [];
  if (DEFENSIVE.test(r.myReply)) flags.push("reply sounds defensive");
  if (r.action === null) flags.push("no action");
  if (r.due === null) flags.push("no date");
  const line = `gap: ${r.said} (from ${r.from})\n    action: ${r.action ?? "____"}\n    due: ${r.due ?? "____"}`;
  return flags.length > 0 ? `${line}\n    ! ${flags.join(", ")}` : line;
}

English

Model solution. Section 13 hands this question to this lesson. Decide it while you are calm, because the day after a disappointing review is the worst day to decide anything. This part makes no claim about how often promotions happen; all of it is advice.

Three signals to write down:

  • Moving goalposts: you close the agreed gaps with evidence and a new requirement appears, more than once.
  • No budget and no date: "no budget" with a date is a plan; with no date it is a holding pattern.
  • No sponsor: nobody in the room where the decision is made will speak for you, and when you ask who would, there's no name.

Then put a date on it. exit-date.ts is a small decision tree with a date on every branch, and it's deliberately strict about order: if you haven't asked in writing, you have no signal at all, so "they don't appreciate me" isn't a decision yet. It's a rough model of your own rules; the point is that you wrote them down first.

Don't threaten to leave. A threat either fails or gets you a promotion nobody wanted to give. If you decide to go, look quietly, and tell them when you have an offer. See lesson 18 for what to do with a counter from your employer.

Leaving well. Your packet is already your next CV and your story bank. Each line becomes a bullet (lesson 02) and a STAR story (lesson 10), and lesson 01 tells you what the external loop will measure. Keep your references good: the people who reviewed your packet are often the ones who would vouch for you later.

// بنحسب بالتاريخ كنص YYYY-MM-DD، والمقارنة النصية صح في الشكل ده
function addMonths(date: string, n: number): string {
  const [y, m, d] = date.split("-").map(Number);
  const target = new Date(Date.UTC(y, m - 1 + n, 1));
  const lastDay = new Date(Date.UTC(target.getUTCFullYear(), target.getUTCMonth() + 1, 0)).getUTCDate();
  target.setUTCDate(Math.min(d, lastDay));
  return target.toISOString().slice(0, 10);
}

function decide(s: Situation): { path: string[]; verdict: string; by: string } {
  const path: string[] = [];
  const stop = (verdict: string, by: string) => ({ path, verdict, by });

  path.push(`asked ${s.askedInWriting ? "yes" : "no"}`);
  if (!s.askedInWriting) return stop("ASK FIRST: you have no signal yet", addMonths(s.today, 1));

  path.push(`closed ${s.gapsClosedWithEvidence ? "yes" : "no"}`);
  if (!s.gapsClosedWithEvidence) return stop("KEEP WORKING on the agreed gaps", s.myDeadline);

  path.push(`moved ${s.barMovedAfterClosing}`);
  if (s.barMovedAfterClosing >= 2) return stop("LOOK OUTSIDE: the bar keeps moving", s.today);

  path.push(`late ${s.today >= s.myDeadline ? "yes" : "no"}`);
  if (s.today >= s.myDeadline) return stop("LOOK OUTSIDE: your own date has passed", s.today);

  path.push(`sponsor ${s.hasSponsor ? "yes" : "no"}`);
  if (!s.hasSponsor) return stop("FIND A SPONSOR: ask who speaks for you in the room", addMonths(s.today, 2));

  path.push(`reason ${s.reason}, date ${s.dateGiven ?? "none"}`);
  if (s.dateGiven === null) return stop("ASK FOR A DATE, in writing", addMonths(s.today, 1));

  return stop("WAIT, and check again on the date they gave", s.dateGiven);
}

❌ The ultimatum

"If I don't get promoted this cycle, I'll have to look elsewhere."

✅ A private date and a written gap

In the meeting: "Can we write down the remaining gap and the date you'll decide?" On your own paper: the date after which you start looking.

بالعامية

❌ "لو ماترقيتش الـ cycle ده، هضطر أدوّر بره": تهديد، يا إما بيفشل، يا إما بيجيبلك ترقية محدش مقتنع بيها. وفي الحالتين علاقتك بقت أوحش.

Cheat sheet · الخلاصة

  • A promotion is an interview: a rubric (the guide), a loop, follow-ups, a decision.
  • Read each guide line as a question and answer it with a story, a link and a number.
  • "Met" with no evidence is an opinion. Keep it as "gap" until something opens.
  • Five cold questions: your part, without you, Senior vs good Mid, what went wrong, who confirms.
  • Numbers: measured, estimated with a method, or not measured. Nothing else.
  • Hard replies ("not yet", "no budget", "more visibility") get a follow-up ending in a gap and a date.
  • Six months of steady evidence beats a pre-review sprint; the six months is advice.
  • Ask specifically, before the cycle, including someone who disagreed. Don't defend in the reply.
  • Signals to leave: moving goalposts, no budget with no date, no sponsor. Decide on a date in advance.
  • Don't threaten. Leave well: the packet becomes your CV and your stories.
بالعامية
  • الترقية interview: فيها rubric (الـ guide)، وloop، وأسئلة متابعة، وقرار.
  • اقرا كل سطر في الـ guide كسؤال، وجاوبه بقصة ولينك ورقم.
  • "met" من غير دليل رأي. خليها "gap" لحد ما يبقى فيه حاجة تتفتح.
  • 5 أسئلة باردة: دورك إنت، من غيرك، Senior ولا Mid كويس، إيه اللي غلط، ومين يأكد.
  • الأرقام: متقاسة، أو تقدير بطريقته، أو متقاستش. ومفيش غير كده.
  • الردود الصعبة ("لسه"، "مفيش budget"، "visibility أكتر") ليها متابعة بتخلص بـ gap وتاريخ.
  • 6 شهور أدلة منتظمة أحسن من sprint قبل الـ review، والـ 6 شهور نصيحة.
  • اطلب بشكل محدد، قبل الـ cycle، ومعاهم حد كنت مختلف معاه. ومتدافعش في الرد.
  • إشارات إنك تمشي: goalposts بتتحرك، مفيش budget ومفيش تاريخ، مفيش sponsor. وحدد تاريخ من بدري.
  • متهددش. وامشي بشكل كويس: الـ packet بيبقى الـ CV والقصص بتاعتك.
